4. **Legal/regulatory.** TCPA automated-voice edge, COPPA conservatism, per-county evidentiary standards UNKNOWN, and the single-catastrophic-contact scenario (DV household) is a company-ending event class. ### What survives
The statutory-artifact insight is correct and dated: Iowa's 15%-plan/20%-referral ladder, Vermont's documented-intervention precondition, Mississippi's funded attendance officers — these convert persuasion problems into document production, which is agent territory. Incumbents have shown no appetite for courtroom-adjacent per-state legal grind. ### Conditions required

4. Do-not-contact registry + human-send gate on all court language from contract #1; tabletop the incident.
5. Explicit kill/pivot test every quarter on SchoolStatus + EDL release notes; if either ships statutory pack generation, retreat to the escalation/legal-evidence layer only — or exit.

The wedge logic (statutory documentation ≠ nudges) survives, but the incumbent clock accelerated twice over since the deep dive: SchoolStatus shipped the investigation first-mile *and* owns a forms/workflow product, and EDL gained Newsela-scale backing. Viable only under the narrow identity above; "AI for attendance" framing is dead on arrival.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. **THE meta-weakness — whitespace defined as incumbent omission, with incumbent response times systematically underestimated.** All five theses locate their opportunity in the layer incumbents "have shown no appetite for." Fresh checks this session show incumbents demonstrably en route in four of five: DegreeSight already sells coursework-routing automation + academic operations + statewide frameworks (DD-06); SchoolStatus shipped the investigation first mile and owns a forms/workflow product one step from statutory packs, while EDL gained Newsela-scale capital (DD-07); Momentive consolidates AMS AI bundling while Medallion/Verifiable prove the agentic playbook one vertical over (DD-08); Adventus APS already occupies the institution-facing phase-2 (DD-09). Only DD-10's consultants are truly static — and they hold the relationships. Meanwhile every thesis's only durable moat (proprietary outcome/rulebook data) compounds over years, and the prescribed funding mechanism — a services-first ladder — consumes exactly the engineering months needed to outrun 12–18-month incumbent windows. Every deep dive prices its own window at 24–36 months; the evidence supports 12–18.
2. **TAM integrity is uniformally weak:** no thesis multiplies a *verified* buyer count by a *verified* price paid for *this* product; every headline number stacks ESTIMATEs, and the two flagged for pressure-testing (DD-08, DD-10) deflate by roughly half under honest assumptions. All five support $2M-ARR boutiques; none credibly supports a venture curve without an unproven expansion bet.
3. **The buyer-quality dilemma has no solution in this set:** the fast-signing buyers (agencies, career colleges, cert bodies) are structurally poor and churny; the rich buyers (districts, universities) buy by committee under post-AllHere/post-Canvas-breach AI distrust. No thesis gets both money and speed.
4. **Services-ladder contradiction (subset of #1, worth naming):** four of five verdicts lean on "service-first de-risks the build," but a 1–5 person team doing services has ~zero slack for the integration/security-review/data-rights work that constitutes the actual moat race. The de-risking mechanism and the winning condition are in direct conflict for a team this small.
