# Indus Valley Civilisation — Art, Architecture & Sculpture (UPSC Art & Culture)

## Chronological Framework

- **Indian cultural history begins ~3200 BC** → Indus Valley Civilisation (IVC)
- **IVC ended ~1500 BC** → followed by **Vedic Period** (no evidence of well-planned cities)
- **Vedic → Mauryan & Post-Mauryan** → **Hindu Temple Architecture** → **Medieval** → **Modern**
- IVC discovered in **1920 by Charles Masson**
- One of 4 great ancient urban civilisations (others: Egypt, Mesopotamia, China)
- Also called **Harappan Civilisation** (emerged ~2500 BC on Indus River banks)
- Spanned large parts of **North-Western & Western India**

---

## Architecture — Town Planning

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Layout** | Rectangular grid pattern |
| **Roads** | North-South & East-West, intersecting at right angles |
| **Main roads** | Divided city into blocks |
| **Lanes** | Smaller lanes connected individual homes to main roads |
| **Building types (3)** | Dwelling houses, Public buildings, Public baths |

### Construction Material
- **Burnt mud bricks** with **standardised dimensions**
- Multi-layered bricks joined with **gypsum mortar**

### City Division
| Zone | Location | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Upper Citadel** | Western part | Granaries, Administrative buildings, Pillared halls, Courtyards |
| **Lower City** | Eastern part | One-room houses (working class quarters), some double-story (stairs found) |

### Granaries
- Designed with **strategic air ducts & raised platforms**
- Purpose: store grain, **protect from pests**

### Great Bath (Mohenjo-daro)
- Most famous public bath
- Surrounded by galleries & rooms
- **No cracks or leaks** → demonstrates advanced engineering
- Indicates **ritualistic cleansing** importance in culture

### Housing Features (Lower City)
- Private wells, bathrooms, proper ventilation
- Double-story houses in some cases

---

## Drainage System — Most Striking Feature

- Small drains from every house → connected to larger drains
- Large drains ran **along main roads**
- **Loose covers** for regular cleaning & maintenance
- **Cesspits at regular intervals** for sewage collection
- Demonstrates exceptional personal & public hygiene priority

---

## Sculptures

- Highly proficient in **3D volumes**
- Most common finds: **Seals, Bronze figures, Pottery**

### 1. Seals

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Shapes** | Mostly square; also triangular, rectangular, circular |
| **Primary material** | **Steatite** (soft stone) — most common |
| **Other materials** | Agate, copper, faience, terracotta; some gold, ivory, copper |
| **Script** | **Pictographic script** — still **undeciphered** |
| **Animal engravings** | **5 common animals**: Unicorn, Hump bull, Rhinoceros, Tiger, Elephant, Buffalo, Bison, Goat, Ibex, Crocodile |
| **Missing animal** | **NO evidence of cow** on any seal |
| **Imaginary creatures** | Half-man half-animal figures |
| **Symbols** | **Swastik-like designs** found |

**Uses of Seals:**
- Primary: **Commercial purposes** & communication
- **Trade**: found in Mesopotamia & Lothal → extensive trade use
- **Amulets/Talisman**: found on dead bodies with holes
- **Education**: some had mathematical images
- **Famous examples**: **Pashupati Seal** (Mohenjo-daro), **Unicorn Seal**

### 2. Bronze Figures — Lost Wax Technique (Cire Perdue)

**Process:**
1. Wax figure created → coated with wet clay
2. Dried → heated → wax melts out through small hole
3. Liquid metal poured into cavity
4. Cooled → clay removed → metal statue matches original wax shape

**Still used today** in many parts of India.

#### Dancing Girl (Mohenjo-daro)
- **World's oldest bronze sculpture**
- **4 inches** tall
- Left arm: bangles | Right arm: amulet & bracelet
- **Tribhanga** dancing posture
- Bronze casting was widely practiced

### 3. Terracotta Figures

- **Fire-baked clay**
- Fewer in number than bronze figures
- Made by **pinching method**
- Mainly found in: **Gujarat & Kalibangan**

---

## Pottery

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Plain pottery** | Household use — grain & water storage |
| **Painted pottery** | **Red & Black pottery**: red background, black designs on top |
| | Themes: Trees, birds, animal figures, geometric patterns |
| **Miniature vessels** | Decoration — excellent craft quality |
| **Perforated pottery** | Possibly for **liquor making** |

- Mostly **fine wheel-made wares**; few hand-made

---

## Ornaments & Fashion

- Materials: **Precious metals, gemstones, bones, baked clay**
- **Both men and women** wore ornaments
- Common: Necklaces, fillets, armlets, finger rings, girdles
- **Only women**: Earrings, anklets
- **Popular beads**: Cornelian, amethyst, quartz, steatite
- **Large-scale bead production**: Factories at **Chanho-daro & Lothal**
- **Fabric**: Cotton & wool (worn by rich and poor)
- **Fashion-conscious**: diverse hairstyles & beards

---

## Stone Sculpture Example

### Bearded Priest (Mohenjo-daro)
- Famous bust — excellent stone figure example
- Wrapped in shawl with **trefoil pattern**
- **Elongated eyes**

---

| Site | Location (Present) | River | Key Findings |
|---|---|---|---|

| **Mehrgarh** | Pakistan | — | **Pre-IVC origin site**, pottery & copper tools |

---

## Key Takeaways

- IVC marks the **earliest urban civilisation** in the Indian subcontinent
- **Scientific town planning**: grid layout, advanced drainage, water supply
- **Metropolitan culture**: brick houses, public baths, granaries
- **Mastery in sculpture**: bronze (lost wax), steatite seals, terracotta, stone
- **Trade network**: seals found in Mesopotamia
- **No cow depiction** on any seal
- **Undeciphered script** — still a mystery
- **Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)** continues excavations
- Drainage system arguably **superior to many modern cities**

# Summary: Mauryan & Post-Mauryan Architecture & Sculpture

---

## Timeline Context

- **Indus Valley Civilization** → ended ~1500 BC
- **Vedic Period** → ~1500 BC to ~500 BC
- **Second Urbanization** → starts ~500 BC → rise of **Buddhism** & **Jainism** (both part of **Śramaṇa tradition** — non-Vedic, opposed varna/jati system; supported by Kshatriya rulers)
- **322 BC** → Mauryan Empire established

---

## Mauryan Art (322 – 185 BC) — Two Categories

| Category | Patronage | Includes |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| **Court Art** (राजकीय) | State initiative | Palaces, pillars, stupas |
| **Popular Art** (जनसामान्य) | Individual initiative | Caves, pottery, sculptures |

---

## 1. Court Art

### Palaces
- First powerful empire in India
- Capitals: **Pataliputra** & **Kumrahar** (both in modern **Bihar**)
- **Chandragupta Maurya's palace** → inspired by **Persepolis** (Achaemenid palace, Iran)
- Principal building material: **Wood**
- **Megasthenes** called it *"greatest creations of mankind"*
- **Ashoka's palace at Kumrahar**: massive structure, **high central pillar**, **3-story wooden structure**, walls decorated with carvings & sculptures

### Ashokan Pillars
| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Height | ~40 ft |
| Material | **Chunar sandstone** |
| Structure | 4 parts: **Shaft (monolith)** → **Capital** (lotus-shaped / bell-shaped) → **Abacus** (circular/rectangular base) → **Animal figure** atop |
| Influence | **Achaemenid** — bell shape & polished lustrous finish |
| Key examples | **Lauria Nandangarh** (Champaran), **Sarnath** (Varanasi) |
| **Sarnath pillar** | Abacus + Animal part = **National Emblem of India** |

### Stupas
- **Vedic origin**: burial mounds for relics/ashes (funerary)
- **Ashoka's reign**: climax of first phase; ~**84,000 stupas** constructed
- Buddhism adopted the tradition

**9 original stupas after Buddha's death:**
Rajgriha, Vaishali, Kapilavastu, Allakappa, Ramagrama, Vethapida, Pava, Kushinagar, Pippalivana

**Stupa Parts:**
- **Core** → unburnt brick
- **Outer surface** → burnt bricks + thick plaster layer
- **Medhi** (terrace) & **Torana** (gateway) → decorated with wooden sculpture
- **Vedika** (railing), **Pradakshina patha** (circumambulatory path for worship)

**Famous stupas:**
- **Sanchi Stupa (MP)** → most famous
- **Piprahwa Stupa (UP)** → oldest

---

## 2. Popular Art

### Rock-Cut Caves
- **First emergence** of rock-cut cave architecture in India
- Used as **Viharas** (living quarters) for Buddhist/Jain monks
- Early caves used by **Ajivika sect** followers
- Later became **Buddhist monasteries**
- Special features: **highly polished interior finish**, **decorative gateways**

**Examples:**
- **Barbar & Nagarjuni Caves (Bihar)** → built 320–185 BC under **Dasharatha** (Ashoka's grandson)

### Sculptures
- Used for: decorating stupa torana/medhi, religious expression
- **Most famous:** **Yaksha & Yakshi** sculptures
- Worshipped in **Hinduism, Jainism, Buddhism**
- **Yakshi** mentioned in **Silapadikaram** (Sangam Tamil text)
- Each **Jain Tirthankara** associated with a **Yakshi**

### Pottery — Northern Black Polished Ware (NBPW)
- **Black paint** + **highly lustrous finish**
- Used as **luxury items**

---

## Post-Mauryan Period (from 2nd century BC)

### Dynasties & Religious Sects

| Region | Dynasties |
|--------|-----------|
| **North** | Shunga, Kanva, Kushana, Shakas |
| **South & West** | Satavahana, Vakataka, Abhira, Vakataka |

**New Brahmanical sects:** Shaivites, Vaishnavites, Shaktas

---

## Post-Mauryan Architecture

### Rock-Cut Caves — Two Types

| Type | Period | Use | Features |
|------|--------|-----|----------|
| **Vihara** | Mauryan (continued) | Residential halls for monks | — |
| **Chaitya** | Post-Mauryan | Prayer halls | Quadrangular chambers, flat roof, open courtyard, stone screen walls (rain protection), decorated with human/animal figures |

**Examples:**
- **Karla Chaitya Hall** (near Mumbai)
- **Ajanta Caves** (Maharashtra): 29 caves — 25 viharas + 4 chaityas
- **Udayagiri & Khandagiri Caves (Odisha)**: built by **Kalinga king Kharavela** (1st–2nd century BC), near Bhubaneswar; both excavated & natural caves; possibly for Jain monks
  - **Udayagiri**: **Hathigumpha inscription** (famous)
  - **Rani Gumpha**: **double-story cave** with beautiful sculptures

### Stupas
- Became **larger** and more **decorative**
- **Stone** replaced wood/brick
- **Shunga dynasty** introduced **Toranas** as beautifully decorated gateways → intricately carved with figures/patterns
- Best examples: **Bharhut Stupa (MP)**, **Sanchi Stupa**

---

## Post-Mauryan Sculpture — Three Major Schools

### 1. Gandhara School (Greco-Indian School of Art)
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Location | Western frontiers of Punjab → modern **Peshawar & Afghanistan** |
| Period | **50 BC – 500 AD** (two stages) |
| Influence | Greek invaders brought **Greek & Roman sculpture traditions** → merged with local |
| Stage 1 | **Blue-grey sandstone** |
| Stage 2 | **Mud & stucco** |

### 2. Mathura School
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Location | Banks of **Yamuna River** |
| Period | **1st–3rd century BC** |
| Influences | Buddhism, Jainism, Hinduism |
| Symbolism | Hindu gods represented by **weapons/attributes** (e.g., Shiva by **linga / mukhalinga**) |
| Buddha's halo | **Larger size**, decorated with **geometric patterns** |
| Bodhisattvas | **Padmapani** (holds lotus) & **Vajrapani** (holds thunderbolt) |

### 3. Amaravati School
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Location | **Krishna & Godavari river valleys** (South India) |
| Patrons | **Satavahana rulers** |
| Focus | **Dynamic images** & **Narrative art** (contrast: Mathura focused on single images) |
| Signature pose | **Tribhanga posture** (body with three bends) |

---

## Conclusion

- **Mauryan**: First state influence in Indian art; **stupa architecture at its zenith**
- **Post-Mauryan**: Smaller dynasties introduced unique features; invaders/bringers also enriched artistic traditions
- Indian art = **story of continuous evolution** — each era reflects its own narratives, designs, and patterns


# Summary: Nagara Temple Architecture (Art & Culture for UPSC)

## Evolution of Temple Architecture in India

- **Vedic Period**: Worship centered around fire (agni)
- **3rd Century BCE**: First stone & wood structures appear
- **4th Century BCE**: Rock-cut cave temples emerge (hill regions)
  - Badami Cave Temples (Karnataka)
  - Udayagiri & Khandagiri Caves (Odisha)
- **5th Century CE (Gupta Period)**: Transition from rock-cut shrines to **structural (freestanding) temples** → Gupta Period = **Golden Age of India**
- Temples evolved into hubs for: education, social gatherings, festivals, marriages, markets, palaces, military garrisons
  - Example: Meenakshi Temple (Madurai), Jagannath Temple (Puri) → emerged as **temple cities**

## Temple Evolution in South India

- **7th Century CE – Pallava Dynasty**: Ruler **Rajasimha Varman** built monuments at **Mamallapuram**
  - Rath Temples, Cave Temples, Krishna Temple, Mahishasura Mardini Mandapa, Shore Temple Complex
  - Declared **UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984**
- **Early 11th–12th Century – Chola Dynasty**: Dravidian architectural marvels
  - **Brihadeshwara Temple** (Thanjavur) – built by Raja Raja I
  - **Gangaikonda Cholapuram Temple** – built by Rajendra I
  - **Ravateshwara Temple** – built by Raja Raja Chola II
  - Collectively called: **Great Living Chola Temples**
- **Regional Styles**: 
  - **Vesara Style** – under Chalukyas of Badami & Hoysalas
  - **Rashtrakutas** (8th Century) – Dravidian-inspired temples, e.g., **Kailash Temple (Ellora)**
- **Late 14th Century – Vijayanagara Empire**: Dominated South India → introduced **Raya Gopuram (ornate gateways)**
  - Vitthala Swami Temple (Hampi)
  - Virupaksha Temple (Hampi)
  - → **Vijayanagara Style**

## Basic Elements of a Hindu Temple

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Garbhagriha** (Sanctum Sanctorum) | Innermost chamber; principal deity installed |
| **Mandapa** | Entrance/assembly hall for worshippers |
| **Shikhar / Vimana** | Mountain-like spire above sanctum; **curvilinear** in North India (Shikhara), **stepped-pyramidal** in South India (Vimana) |
| **Amalaka** | Stone disk atop the Shikhara (mostly North Indian temples) |
| **Kalasha** | Topmost part resting on amalaka (common in North India) |
| **Antarala** | Narrow passage connecting garbhagriha and mandapa |
| **Jagati** | Raised platform in Northern temples (devotees sit & pray) |
| **Vahana** | Vehicle/mount of the main deity; positioned directly in front of garbhagriha |

## Classification of Temple Architecture

Three broad types:
1. **Nagara Style** (Northern)
2. **Dravidian Style** (Southern)
3. **Vesara Style** (Hybrid / Deccan)

---

## Nagara Style – Detailed Breakdown

### General Features (5th Century CE onwards)
- **Geographical extent**: From Himalayas to Vindhya ranges
- Evolved during **Gupta Period** in **5 stages**:

| Stage | Example | Key Features |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| **1st Stage** | Temple No. 17, Sanchi | Flat roof, square-shaped, low platforms, shallow pillars |
| **2nd Stage** | Parvati Temple, Nachna Kuthura (MP) | Raised platforms, covered ambulatory passage (pradakshina path) |
| **3rd Stage** | Dashavatara Temple, Deogarh (UP) | Shikhara developed instead of flat roof; **Panchayatana style** |
| **4th Stage** | — | Square main temple with longitudinal projections |
| **5th Stage** | — | Circular-shaped temples with rectangular projections |

### Key Architectural Features of Nagara Style
- Built on elevated platforms
- **Panchayatana style** ground plan (main shrine + 4 subsidiary shrines)
- Mandapa (assembly hall) built directly in front of principal shrine
- Garbhagriha directly under the tallest Shikhara
- Images of **Ganga & Yamuna** goddesses carved outside garbhagriha
- Shikhara can be single or multiple
- **3 Types of Shikhara**:
  1. **Rekha Prasad / Latina** – curvilinear
  2. **Phamsana** – pyramidal
  3. **Valabhi** – wagon-vaulted
- Fluted disc (**Amalaka**) atop Shikhara; spherical **Kalasha** topping amalaka
- Inner walls divided into **3 vertical planes** called **Ratha** (triratha, pancharatha, saptaratha, navaratha exist)
- Narrative sculptures on walls
- Garbhagriha enclosed by **covered pradakshina patha** (ambulatory passage)
- **No water tanks or gateways** (unlike Dravidian style)

---

## Nagara Style by Region

### 1. Central India (UP, MP, Rajasthan)
- Material: **Sandstone**
- Classical examples:

#### (a) Dashavatara Vishnu Temple, Deogarh (UP)
- Late Gupta Period; classical example
- **Panchayatana style**
- **Rekha Prasad Shikhara**
- **West-facing** temple (majority are North/East-facing)
- Vishnu depicted in 3 forms on walls:
  - **South wall**: Sheshshayana (reclining on Shesha)
  - **East wall**: Nara-Narayana
  - **West wall**: Gajendra Moksha
- Subsidiary shrines also Vishnu forms → hence called **"Dashavatara"**

#### (b) Khajuraho Temples (MP)
- Built **10th Century** by **Chandela rulers**
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site**
- Dedicated to **Hindu & Jain faiths**
- **64 Yogini Temple** → dedicated to Yoginis (part of Tantric worship)
- Key temples:
  - **Kandariya Mahadeva Temple** – dedicated to Lord Shiva
  - **Lakshmana Temple** – grandest in Khajuraho; established by Chandela King **Dhanga** in **954 CE**

### 2. Western India (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Western MP)
- Material: **Sandstone**
- Built under **Solanki (Chalukya) rulers**

#### Modhera Sun Temple (Gujarat)
- Built by Solanki king **Bhima Deva I**
- Key feature: **Surya Kund** – rectangular stepped tank (sacred water body)
- **Torana** (decorated gateway) leads to assembly hall
- Central shrine: **wall plane** (no pillars inside)
- **East-facing** → during equinox, sun rays fall directly on central shrine

### 3. Eastern India (North-East, Odisha, Bengal)

#### (a) Assam
- **10th Century**: Gupta influence visible
- **12th–14th Century**: **Ahom style** developed in Guwahati
  - Ahom = mixture of **Burmese + Pala style (Bengal)**
- **Kamakhya Temple (Guwahati)** – Shakti Peetha dedicated to Goddess Kamakhya

#### (b) Bengal
- **9th–11th Century**: **Pala rulers** (Buddhist patrons) → **Pala style**
  - Built viharas, chaityas, stupas
- **11th–13th Century**: **Sena rulers**
  - Material: **Terracotta bricks** (mixture of clay)
  - **Curved / sloping roof** → **Bangla roof** (later adopted by Mughals)
  - Figures highly polished → **metallic & lustrous** appearance

#### (c) Odisha (Kalinga Style)
- Distinct **Kalinga Style**
- **Shikhara**: almost vertical with sudden inward curve at top
- **Mandapa (Jagmohan)** directly in front of the deul (shrine)
- **Exterior walls**: intricately carved; **Interior walls**: plain
- Temple surrounded by **boundary walls**
- Key examples:
  - **Jagannath Temple (Puri)**
  - **Lingaraja Temple (Bhubaneswar)**
  - **Sun Temple (Konark)** – finest example
    - Built **1240 CE** by **Eastern Ganga Dynasty** king **Narasimha Deva I**
    - Built on raised platform; detailed carvings
    - **12 pairs of giant wheels** sculpted → represent Sun God's chariot
    - **UNESCO World Heritage Site**
    - Featured on **reverse side of ₹10 currency note**

## Key Takeaways
- Nagara Style = Northern Indian temple architecture (Himalayas to Vindhyas)
- Evolved from simple flat-roof squares to complex curvilinear shikharas
- 3 shikhara types: Latina/Rekha Prasad, Phamsana, Valabhi
- **Panchayatana** = main shrine + 4 subsidiary shrines
- Walls divided into vertical **Rathas** (triratha to navaratha)
- Ganga-Yamuna goddess figures outside garbhagriha
- No water tanks or gateways (unlike Dravidian)
- Major dynasties: **Gupta, Chandela, Solanki, Pala, Sena, Eastern Ganga**
- Major sites: **Deogarh, Khajuraho, Modhera, Konark, Bhubaneswar, Puri, Guwahati**


# Dravidian & Vesara Temple Architecture — Summary Notes

## DRAVIDIAN STYLE (Southern India)

**Region:** Krishna River → Kanyakumari
**Key Features:**
- Compound wall enclosing the temple
- **Gopuram** — intricately designed entrance gateway
- **Vimana** — stepped pyramidal shape (equivalent of Nagara *shikhara*)
- **Shikhara** (in Dravidian) = the crowning element (equivalent of Nagara *kalasha*)
- **Dwarapala** — guardian sculptures at garbhagriha entrance
- Water tank inside the compound
- Subsidiary shrines inside/outside main tower
- **Garbhagriha** in the smallest, oldest tower
- Additional boundary walls added as temple-town population grew
- **Example:** Sri Ranganathar Temple, Srirangam (Tiruchirappalli) — 7 concentric rectangular enclosure walls, each with a gopuram; main shrine at center

---

## PALLAVA ARCHITECTURE (4 stages)

| Stage | Period | Ruler | Key Development |
|-------|--------|-------|----------------|
| **1st** | 610–630 CE | Mahendravarman I | **Rock-cut temples** → called **Mandapas** |
| **2nd** | 630–668 CE | Narasimhavarman I | Rock-cut temples decorated with sculptures; Mandapas divided into **Rathas** (monolithic chariot temples). Large = **Dharmaraja Ratha**, Small = **Draupadi Ratha** |
| **3rd** | 690–800 CE | Rajasimhavarman | **Real structural temples** (first free-standing stone temples) |
| **4th** | 800–900 CE | Nandivarman | Final stage — temples were much smaller in size |

### Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram)
- **Ancient port city** under Pallavas
- UNESCO World Heritage Site (1984)
- **Key Monument:** Pancha Rathas (Five Rathas) — although neighboring temples are rock-cut, these were built using **granite** → considered **earliest structural temple in South India**
- Contains shrines to **Shiva & Vishnu**
- **Unique feature:** 3 main shrines — one Shiva shrine east-facing, one Shiva shrine west-facing, one shrine dedicated to **Ananta Sayana Vishnu** (reclining Vishnu)
- Outer walls have **Nandi sculptures**
- **Site of 2nd India-China Informal Summit (2019)** — PM Modi & Xi Jinping

---

## CHOLA STYLE (part of Dravidian tradition)

- Incorporated Pallava features with variations
- **Shaivism** dominant

### Brihadeshwara Temple (Thanjavur)
- **Dedicated to:** Shiva (as Lingam in double-storey sanctum)
- **Built by:** Rajaraja Chola
- **Year:** 1010 CE
- **Significance:** Largest & tallest Indian temple; architectural grandeur
- **Vimana:** Pyramidal, ~70m high; topped with **dome-shaped shikara** with Nandi images; crowned by **3m 8cm tall kalasha**
- **Decorations:** Murals & sculptures of mythological stories on surrounding walls

### Gangaikonda Cholapuram Temple
- **Built by:** Rajendra I
- **Year:** 1035 CE
- **Shape:** Square plan
- **Dedicated to:** Shiva; also depicts Vishnu, Durga, Surya, Harihara
- **Considered:** South India's largest Shiva temple (along with Brihadeshwara)

### Airavateshwara Temple (Thanjavur)
- **Built by:** Rajaraja Chola II
- **Century:** 12th Century CE
- **Dedicated to:** Shiva
- **Key detail:** Mandapa pillars intricately carved with stories — Parvati's penance, Shiva's marriage, Shiva's fight with Asuras
- **Miniature panels:** Depict stories of **63 Nayanars (Shaiva saints)** → evidence of Chola belief in Shaivism

> **UNESCO:** All three Chola temples declared World Heritage Sites in 1987 (as "Great Living Chola Temples")

---

## VESARA STYLE (Deccan / Karnataka Style)

**Period:** Mid-7th Century CE
**Nature:** **Hybrid** of Nagara + Dravidian styles
**Borrowed from Nagara:** Curvilinear shikhara + square base
**Borrowed from Dravidian:** Stepped shikhara + intricate carvings

**Three Dynasties that developed Vesara:**
1. **Chalukyas** (Badami & Kalyani)
2. **Rashtrakutas**
3. **Hoysalas**

### Key Examples

#### Pattadakal Temples
- **UNESCO World Heritage Site**
- **10 temples total:** 4 Nagara style, 4 Dravida style, 1 Jain temple, 1 Papanatha temple (Vesara style)
- **Virupaksha Temple:** Built by Queen Lokamahadevi (wife of Vikramaditya II) in 733 CE — Dravidian style

#### Aihole
- **Durga Temple** — classical Vesara example
- **Khan Temple** — classical Vesara example

---

## HOYSALA ARCHITECTURE (Vesara sub-style)

**Period:** 1050–1300 CE
**Prominence:** After decline of Cholas & Pandyas
**Locations:** Belur, Somnathpur, Halebidu
**Distinctive Features:**
- **Panchayatana style** predominantly adopted
- **Star-shaped (stellate) ground plan** — new evolution by Hoysalas
- **Main building material:** Chloritic schist (soapstone)

**Key Temple:** Hoysaleshwara Temple (Halebidu) — classical example

---

## RASHTRAKUTA ARCHITECTURE

**Founded by:** Dantidurga (735 CE)
**Capital:** Manyakheta (Malkhed)
**Religious tolerance:** Patronized all religions; adopted Pallava style

### Kailash Temple (Ellora, Aurangabad, Maharashtra)
- **Builder:** Krishna I (Rashtrakuta)
- **Century:** 8th Century CE
- **Context:** Ellora Caves — 34 caves total (Buddhist, Jain, Hindu); Kailash is the **largest Hindu cave temple**
- **Construction:** Carved from **single monolith** top-down
- **Labor:** ~7,000 workers, >100 years
- **Dimensions:** 276 ft long × 154 ft wide
- **Shape:** Modeled on Mount Kailash
- **Entrance:** Small gopuram with life-size elephants
- **Subsidiary shrines:** 5 around main temple — dedicated to Ganga, Yamuna, Saraswati
- **Main temple:** 3-storey with sabhagriha, Nandi mandapa, garbhagriha (Shivalinga)
- **Notable carvings:** Ardhanarishwara, Veerabhadra (Shiva), Ravana shaking Mount Kailash, Ramayana & Mahabharata stories
- **Significance:** Considered the **climax of rock-cut temple architecture in India**

### Elephanta Caves (Rashtrakuta)
- **Total caves:** 7 — 2 Buddhist (east) + 5 rock-cut Hindu (west)
- **Main temple:** Dedicated to Lord Shiva
- **Entrance:** 7m high **Sadashiva (Trimurti)** — depicts Shiva's 3 forms: Creator, Preserver, Destroyer
- **Sculpture:** **Vishala Mahesha Murti** — Shiva center, Uma & Bhairava on sides

---

## VIJAYANAGARA STYLE

**Capital:** Hampi, Karnataka
**Influences:** Chola + Hoysala + Pandya + Chalukya + Islamic architecture of Bijapur
**Key Features:**
- **Massive gopurams (Raya Gopurams)** — built on all 4 sides
- Multiple mandapas; **central mandapa** = **Kalyana Mandapa**
- **Secular buildings** concept emerged

**Hampi — UNESCO World Heritage Site**
**Key Temples:**
- Vitthala Swami Temple
- Lotus Mahal
- Virupaksha Temple
- Raghunatha Temple
- **Narasimha on Sesha (snake) idol** — architectural beauty

---

## ROLE OF TEMPLES (Socio-Economic-Artistic)

**Positive:**
- **Employment hub:** Artisans, craftsmen, priests, cooks, musicians, dancing girls
- **Urbanization catalyst:** Commercial activities → markets → inland trade boost
- **Land grants (Devadana):** Temples received land gifts
- **Agriculture:** Water tanks, canals, land reclamation
- **Education:** Traditional subjects (Vedas, grammar, logic) + Mathematics, Astronomy, Astrology taught; schools & colleges attached to temples; **free boarding & lodging** for students
- **Artistic hub:** Musicians & dancers appreciated; art passed generationally
- **Example:** Kanchipuram — Uttara Merur inscription mentions educational functions

**Negative:**
- **Perpetuated caste system:** Brahmins given religious exclusivity
- **Devadasi system:** Girls dedicated to deity worship & service; initially high social status → later exploited for menial jobs (temple cleaning) & sexual exploitation by upper-caste men
- **Present status:** Devadasi system banned as social evil

# Summary: Islamic Art & Architecture (Medieval India) — Aadesh Singh, StudyIQ

## Key Term
- **Indo-Islamic Architecture** (aka **Indo-Saracenic Architecture**): Fusion of Persian & Indian styles after Islamic rule in India.

---

## Features of Indo-Islamic Architecture
- **Arches & Domes** gained prominence — called **Arcuate style**
- **Minars** (towers) built around mosques & assembly halls
- **Sculptures avoided** (against Quran); replaced by **Calligraphy** as decoration
- **Arabesque** patterns: vegetation (flowers/leaves) in geometric stem-forms
- **Geometry** heavily used as design principle
- **Intricate Jali (lattice) work** — shows importance of light in Islam
- **Water features**: religious, cooling & decorative purposes
- **Charbagh style**: square block divided into 4 identical gardens
- **Pietra dura**: inlaying precious stones into stone walls
- **Foreshortening technique**: inscriptions designed for depth-perception

---

## Delhi Sultanate Period (1206–1526 CE)

### A. Imperial Style (Delhi Rulers)

#### 1. Slave Dynasty (1206–1290) — Mamluk Style
- Mostly **remodeled existing Hindu structures**
- **Qutub Minar** (5-storey):
  - Ground floor — Qutubuddin Aibak (initiated)
  - Next 3 floors — Iltutmish (completed)
  - 5th floor — Firoz Shah Tughlaq
- **Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque** (Qutub Minar premises)
- **Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra** (Ajmer)

#### 2. Khilji Dynasty (1290–1320) — Seljuk Style
- **Red sandstone** hallmark
- **Arcuate style** began here
- Large-scale use of **mortar**
- Monuments: **Alai Darwaza** (by Alauddin Khilji), **Siri Fort**

#### 3. Tughlaq Dynasty — "Crisis period" for architecture
- **Grey sandstone** used
- Focus on **strength**, less on decoration
- **Sloping walls** for strength
- Arch + lintel both used for entrances
- Monuments: **Tomb of Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq**, **Tughlaqabad**, **Jahanpanah**, **Firozabad**

#### 4. Lodhi Dynasty — Architectural decline, only tombs built
- **Double Dome** introduced:
  - Outer dome — aesthetics
  - Inner hollow dome — internal symmetry
- Tombs: **solid, no decoration**, **octagonal shape**
- **Lodhi Gardens**; **City of Agra** (by Sikandar Lodhi)

---

### B. Provincial Schools of Architecture

#### 1. Bengal School
- **Bricks** & **Black marble** main materials
- **Sloping Bangla roofs** (continued from temple tradition)
- Monuments: **Kadam Rasul Mosque** (Gaur), **Adina Mosque** (Pandua)

#### 2. Malwa / Mandu School (aka **Pathan School**)
- Different colored stones & marbles
- **Large windows** with arches & pillars
- **No minars**
- Famous for **environmental adaptation**: ventilation, large windows, artificial reservoirs, local materials
- Monuments: **Jahaz Mahal**, **Rani Roopmati Pavilion**, **Ashrafi Mahal**

#### 3. Jaunpur School (aka **Sharqi Style**)
- Patronized by **Sharqi rulers**
- **No minars**
- Bold & forceful painted characters on sides & center of prayer halls
- Monument: **Atala Mosque** (Jaunpur)

#### 4. Bijapur School (aka **Deccan Style**)
- Patron: **Adil Shah**
- **Three-arch facade** & **Bulbous domes**
- Focus on **cornice decoration**
- **Iron clamps** + strong mortar for strength
- Walls decorated with **rich carvings**
- Monuments: **Gol Gumbaz**, **Adil Shah's Majlis**

---

## Mughal Architecture (1526–)

- **Peak of Indo-Islamic Architecture** — greatest exchange of ideas & styles

### Babur (1526–1530)
- Built mosques in **Panipat & Rohilkhand** (1526)
- **Kabuli Bagh Mosque** (Panipat) — after victory over Ibrahim Lodhi
- Introduced **Charbagh garden style**
- Short reign — no major new styles/techniques

### Humayun (1530–1540, 1555–1556)
- Continuous power struggle with Sher Shah — could not focus on art
- Founded city **Din Panah** (unfinished)
- **Persian style** remained prominent

### Sher Shah Suri (1539–1555) — Sur Dynasty
- **Qila-e-Kuhna** (Purana Qila, Delhi)
- **Rohtas Fort** (Pakistan)
- **Sher Shah Suri Mosque** (Patna) — **Afghan style**
- His reign = **transition from Lodhi to Mughal style**
- Renovated ancient road → renamed **Sadak-e-Azam** → later **Grand Trunk Road**
- Built **serais (rest houses)** for travelers
- **Tomb at Sasaram** (Bihar, Rohtas district): red sandstone, situated in a lake

### Akbar (1556–1605) — Golden Period begins
- **Red sandstone** principal material
- Introduced **True Arch / Four-centered Arch**
- **Agra Fort**:
  - Started under Akbar; most interior structures under Shah Jahan
  - Key buildings: **Moti Masjid**, **Diwan-e-Aam**, **Diwan-e-Khas**, **Jahangiri Mahal**, **Sheesh Mahal**
  - Gardens in **Charbagh style**
- **Fatehpur Sikri** — capital city, "frozen moment in history" (unique Hindu + Persian mix)
  - **Buland Darwaza** (40m, red sandstone, 1576 CE) — Akbar's Gujarat victory; world's largest gateway
  - **Tomb of Salim Chishti** (1581) — white marble, exquisite jali work, arabesque walls
  - **Panch Mahal** — 5-storey columnar structure, concept of Persian **badgir (wind catcher)**
  - **Jodhabai Palace** & **Mariam-uz-Zamani Palace** — interior motifs of bells & flowers (Hindu)
  - **Ibadat Khana** — interfaith discussions
  - **Pachisi Court** — where Akbar played chess
  - **Hiran Minar** — memorial for Akbar's favorite elephant Hiran; also served as lighthouse

### Jahangir (1605–1627)
- More focused on **painting** than architecture
- **Akbar's Tomb** (Sikandra)
- **Jahangir's own tomb** (Lahore)
- **Shalimar Bagh** (Kashmir)
- **Moti Masjid** (Lahore)
- **White marble replaced red sandstone** as chief building material
- **Tomb of Itimad-ud-Daula** (built by Nur Jahan):
  - First fully white marble Mughal structure
  - Finest **pietra dura** & **low-relief carvings** (on plain background)

### Shah Jahan (1628–1658)
- **Taj Mahal** (mentioned as reference point)
- **Red Fort** (Delhi)
- **Jama Masjid** (Delhi)
- **Shalimar Bagh** (Lahore)
- **Shahjahanabad** city
- **Peacock Throne** — finest of the period

### Aurangzeb (1658–1707) & After
- **Decline** of Mughal architecture
- **Muhammad Azam Shah** (last Mughal to build):
  - **Bibi Ka Maqbara** (Aurangabad) — mother Rabia Daurani's tomb, called "poor imitation of Taj Mahal"
  - **Zeenat Mahal** (Delhi)

---

## Conclusion
- Islamic architecture in India began with Delhi Sultanate, saw ups & downs
- **Mughal architecture** = peak / **zenith of Islamic art** in India
- Greatest exchange of ideas & styles between local & foreign traditions
- Key elements: arches, vaults, domes, ornamental decoration
- Mughal architecture also synthesized **Deccan & regional styles**

## 1. Portuguese Influence

- **Style introduced:** Iberian Style (pre-dates Roman art; originated in Spain & Portugal)
- **Later style:** Baroque Style (developed in late 16th-century Europe for dramatic church interiors; used elaborate details, contrasting dark/light colours)

## 2. French Influence

- **Key contribution:** Introduced **urban city planning**
- **Style:** Cartesian Grid Plan + Scientific architectural designs
- **Concept introduced:** **Anonymous Architecture** — building fronts are simple, no ornamentation/design
- **Cities developed:** Pondicherry (Puducherry), Chandernagore (West Bengal), Mahé (Kerala), Karaikal (Tamil Nadu)

## 3. British Influence

- Most **dominant colonial power** → most significant architectural impact.

### 3A. Indo-Gothic Style (a.k.a. Victorian Style)

- **Fusion of:** Indian (native) elements + Gothic Style
- **Characteristics:**
  - Extremely large & elaborate constructions
  - Walls were **thinner** compared to Indo-Islamic constructions
  - **Pointed arches** (not curving arches)
  - **Large windows** — unique feature
  - Church floor plan = **Crucifixion (cross) shape**
  - First use of **steel I-beams & filled concrete**
- **Also called:** Victorian Style

### 3B. Neo-Roman Style (a.k.a. Neo-Classical Style)

- Emerged **after 1911** (when capital shifted to Delhi)
- **Also called:** Rome of Hindustan
- **Key architects:** **Edwin Lutyens** & **Herbert Baker** — designed New Delhi
- **Characteristics:**
  - Anonymous style (no interesting ornamentation/design)
  - **Hybrid nature** — mixed all architectural styles → eclectic look
  - Simplicity, modernity, and utility sometimes compromised
  - **Focus on circular buildings**
  - Introduced **Up-Turn Dome** (purely ornamental/decoration feature; no structural importance)
    - Example: Supreme Court of India, Rashtrapati Bhavan

# Indian Paintings – Part 1 | StudyIQ | Aadesh Singh

---

## Evolution of Indian Paintings

- **Prehistoric Period** (50,000 BCE – 6,000 BCE) → Paleolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic, Chalcolithic
- Earliest evidence: **Bhimbetka Caves** (Bhopal, MP) – 600+ painted caves
- Paintings engraved on floor/roof/walls = **Murals**
- Prehistoric rock paintings = **Petroglyphs**
- Key prehistoric sites: **Bhimbetka** (MP), **Pachmarhi** (MP), **Mirzapur** (UP)
- Later: **Fresco paintings** – wet plaster + pure colors, religious/spiritual themes

## Sadanga – Six Principles of Indian Painting (Vatsyayana, Kamasutra, Gupta Period)

| # | Term | Meaning |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | **Rupa-bheda** | Appearance / understanding of form |
| 2 | **Pramanam** | Correct proportion, measurement & structure |
| 3 | **Bhava** | Mood / emotional expression of figures |
| 4 | **Lavanya-yojana** | Artistic grace / grace rendered artistically |
| 5 | **Sadrishya** | Similarity / likeness |
| 6 | **Varna-kabhang** | Artistic use of brush & colors |

- Gupta Period murals & frescos: finest examples in **Ajanta & Ellora Caves** (Buddha's life, religious/mythological scenes)

## Medieval Period – New Schools Emerge

- **Mughal, Rajput, Deccani** styles originate
- **Modern Period** (19th century – present) → Western influence → **Bengal School**, **Progressive Artists' Society**

## Four Categories of Indian Paintings

1. **Mural** – walls/solid structures
2. **Miniature** – small medium, intricate
3. **Modern** – post-19th century, Western influence
4. **Folk** – regional/traditional

---

## MURAL PAINTINGS

- Origin: ~10th century BCE – 10th century CE
- Natural or man-made caves
- Themes: **Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism**
- Key sites: Ajanta, Ellora, Bagh, Badami, Arma Malai, Ravana Chhaya, Sittanavasal, Kailashnath Temple

### 1. Ajanta Caves (Maharashtra, Aurangabad)
- **UNESCO World Heritage** | Sahyadri ranges, Wagora river
- **200 BCE – 650 CE** | 29 caves, **horseshoe** layout
- Associated dynasties: **Maurya, Gupta, Shunga**
- Theme: **Buddhist**
- Bold **red outlines** for figures; pigments still vibrant
- Famous paintings: **Padmapani, Vajrapani, Manjushri, Dying Princess, Apsara**

### 2. Ellora Caves (100 km from Ajanta)
- 100 caves (34 open): **17 Hindu, 12 Buddhist, 5 Jain**
- **Kailashnath Temple** – world's largest rock-cut Hindu temple; built by **Rashtrakuta ruler Krishna I**
- Murals in **5 caves**; created in **two stages** (during carving + centuries later)
- Distinctive features: twisted heads, bent arms, plump limbs, pointed noses, bulging eyes

### 3. Bagh Caves (Madhya Pradesh)
- 9 Buddhist caves
- Technique: **orange mud plaster + lime** coating over walls & ceilings
- Similar to Ajanta but with **thicker colors, bolder outlines, highly modeled figures**
- Themes show **secularism**

### 4. Badami Caves (Bagalkot, Karnataka)
- **6th century** rock-cut temples
- Large figures of **Vishnu avatars**; fully adorned with mural paintings
- **One of India's oldest murals**
- Figures: **graceful, compassionate, sensitive expressions**
- Similar to Ajanta & Bagh but more refined
- **UNESCO World Heritage**

### 5. Other Mural Sites
| Site | Location |
|------|----------|
| Arma Malai Caves | Tamil Nadu |
| Sittanavasal Caves | Tamil Nadu |
| Ravana Chhaya Rock Shelter | Odisha |
| Lepakshi Temple | Andhra Pradesh |
| Jogimara Caves | Chhattisgarh |

---

## MINIATURE PAINTINGS

- Emerged after **paper invention** – shift from walls to small media
- Size: **never > 25 sq inches**
- Medium: **paper, palm leaves, fabric**
- Known for: intricate detailing, first-class brushwork
- Themes: **religious & non-religious Indian literature**
- **Five sub-categories**

### A. Early Miniature Paintings

#### Pala School of Painting
- **Oldest miniature style** in India
- Theme: **Buddhist**
- Reflects **Nalanda & Vikramashila** heritage
- Medium: **palm leaves**; **light colors**, **sinuous/curved lines**
- Famous work: **Ashtasahasrika Prajnaparamita** (Buddhist manuscript)

#### Apabhramsa School of Art
- Region: **Gujarat, Rajasthan, Malwa**
- Period: **11th – 15th century**
- Initially **Jain** → later **Vaishnava** influence
- Works include: **Gitagovinda** (Radha-Krishna) + Jain iconography
- Famous manuscripts: **Kalpasutra**, **Kalkacharya Katha**

### B. Transition Period Miniature (14th century – "Phase of Change")
- **Islamic influence** due to Muslim rulers in North India
- **Vijayanagara Empire** (South) → new style similar to **Deccani**
- Features:
  - **Rich colors, bold black outlines**
  - Faces at **three-quarter angle** → 3D appearance
  - Detailed backgrounds: rivers, mountains, trees, animals

### C. Delhi Sultanate Miniature (14th century)
- **Persian elements + Indian touch**
- Paintings for **manuscript illustrations**
- Example: **Nimmat Nama** (Nasir Shah's reign)
- Laid foundation for **Mughal, Rajput & Deccan schools**

### D. Mughal Era Miniature Paintings

**General features:**
- Heavy **Persian influence**
- Shift from religious themes → **court scenes, hunting, historical events, royal life**
- **Naturalistic style** (Persian)
- **Bright, shiny colors**
- **Geometry principles** → special visual effect

#### Mughal Rulers & Patronage

| Ruler | Key Contribution |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Babur** | Patronized Persian artist **Bihzad**; commissioned painted **family tree album** – foundation of Mughal painting |
| **Humayun** | Met **Abdus Samad & Mir Sayyid Ali** in Persia; brought them to India; **Tutinama** manuscript |
| **Akbar** | Established **Tasvir Khana** (formal art studio); salaried Indian & Persian artists; realistic paintings, dramatic expressions, 3D figures, calligraphy. **Famous artists**: Daswanth, Basawan, Kesu. **Works**: Tutinama, Hamzanama, Anwar-i-Suhayli, Gulistan of Saadi |
| **Jahangir** | **Golden Period** of Mughal Miniature. Preference: **nature paintings** (animals, birds, plants), **portraits**. Naturalism & realism. Decorated margins/frames. **Famous painter**: Ustad Mansur. **Works**: Zebra portrait, Turkey Cock, Ayare Danish album |
| **Shah Jahan** | **Artificial elements** over naturalism; **European influence**. Used pen, pencil, sketch, charcoal; heavy **gold & silver**. **Painters**: Mohammad Nadir Samarqandi, Mir Hashim |

# Indian Paintings — Part 2 | Summary Notes

---

## 1. Decline of Mughal Miniature Painting

- **Aurangzeb**, an orthodox Islamic ruler, stopped patronizing painters
- Painters migrated to other kingdoms → Mughal painting declined
- Miniature painting's final category: **Regional Miniatures** (covered in next lecture)

---

## 2. Regional Schools of Miniature Painting

Developed under the patronage of regional kingdoms.

### A. Rajasthan School of Painting (aka Rajput School of Painting)

- Originated & developed in Rajput kingdoms of Rajasthan
- Influenced by Mughal miniatures
- **Major Sub-Schools:**

#### i. Mewar School
- Based on **Vaishnavism** themes
- Subjects: Ramayana, Bhagavata Purana, popular poems & stories like **Rasika Priya** and **Ragamala**
- Famous painter: **Sahib Din**
- Later shift to secular themes: darbar scenes, royal ceremonies
- **Tamasha paintings** also popular

#### ii. Jaipur School (aka Dhundar School)
- Development largely due to **Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh**
- Examples found on palace walls and **Amer Mahal** cenotaphs
- Themes similar to Mewar School

#### iii. Marwar School
- Developed under **Maharaja Man Singh**
- Covers Jodhpur & Bikaner areas
- Themes: Vaishnavism + **Shaivism** (Shiv Purana, Nat Charitra, Durga Charitra, Panchatantra)
- Unique features: colorful clothing of figures, bright colors
- **Two sub-schools:**
  - **Kishangarh School**
    - Mainly Radha-Krishna paintings
    - Unique features: long necks, arched eyebrows, thin lips, wide eyes
    - Famous painting: **Bani Thani**
    - Famous painters: **Savant Singh**, **Nagari Das**, **Nihal Chand**
  - **Bundi School**
    - Known for **naturalism** and **realism**
    - Themes: Lord Krishna's life

#### iv. Kota School
- Kota & Bundi styles share similarities → together called **Hadoti School of Miniature Painting**
- Themes: Krishna bhakti, **tiger & bear hunting**
- Kota & Bundi paintings have strong resemblance

---

### B. Pahari School of Miniature Painting

- Developed in Himalayan hill states
- Subjects: mythological stories to Indian literature
- Multiple figures on canvas, all energetic & distinct
- **Two greatest painters: Nainsukh and Mank**
- **Two major sub-schools:**

#### i. Basohli School
- First patron: **Raja Kripal Pal**
- Unique features: **bold & dark colors**
- Based on famous poems
- Famous works: **Ras Manjari** (painter Devidas), **Geet Govindam** (painter Mank)

#### ii. Kangra School
- Known for **delicate softness** and **naturalistic appearance**
- Most famous painting: Portrait of **Maharaja Sansar Chand of Kangra** (by descendants of Nainsukh)

---

### C. Miniature Painting Styles of South India

- Different from North Indian miniatures
- **Spiritualism dominates** themes (not royal life)
- Beautiful depictions of Hindu gods & goddesses
- **Extensive use of gold**
- **Major sub-schools:**

#### i. Tanjore School
- Patronized by **Marathas**
- Biggest contribution: **Maharaja Sarfoji II**
- Painted on **glass or wooden plaques** using bright colors

#### ii. Mysore School
- Two or more figures in one painting
- One figure is dominant in **size and color**

---

## 3. Modern Indian Paintings

### A. Company Paintings

- Developed during colonial rule as amalgamation of Rajput, Mughal, Pahari, South Indian & **European elements**
- British painters in India blended Indian paintings with European elements
- Followed in: Kolkata, Chennai, Delhi, Patna, Varanasi, Thanjavur
- **Features:**
  - First use of **water colors**
  - Modern painting & **shading techniques**
  - Themes: Indian landscapes, **flora & fauna**
- Famous painters: **Mazhar Ali Khan**, **Ghulam Ali Khan**

### B. Bazaar Paintings

- European influence visible but different from Company Paintings
- Fusion of European techniques/themes with Indian techniques/themes
- **Roman & Greek elements** also seen
- Prevalent in **Bihar & Bengal**
- Greco-Roman heritage + Indian bazaar scenes with European background
- Religious subjects were painted, but **Ganesha-like deities prohibited** (deviated from European natural human form)

### C. Bengal School

- Developed to counter European influence & revive indigenous Indian techniques
- Story begins **early 20th century**
- **Abanindranath Tagore** launched his painting album **"Arabian Nights"**
- Features: Simple colors, reflection of **Swadeshi values**
- Iconic works:
  - **Bharat Mata** (Abanindranath Tagore)
  - **Mahatma Gandhi sketch** (Nandalal Bose)

### D. Raja Ravi Varma

- **Father of Modern Indian Paintings**
- Fused South Indian painting elements with **Western painting & color techniques**
- Themes: mythological stories & literary works
- Famous paintings: *Lady in the Moonlight*, *Shakuntala*, *Damayanti*, *Swan*, *Ravana Kidnapping Sita*

### E. Cubist Style of Painting (India)

- Inspired by **European Cubist movement**
- Uses **modern art techniques** + geometry principles to show objects from every angle
- Perfect balance of geometry & colors
- Famous painter: **M.F. Husain**
  - His work: **"Personification of Romance"** — beautiful example of cubist form & abstract style

### F. Progressive Artists' Group (PAG)

- Formed in **1947** by a local group of painters
- Bold colors on progressive themes
- Based on **abstract art forms** & soft themes
- **Founder: Francis Newton Souza**
- Eminent members: **S.H. Raza**, **H.A. Gade**, **M.F. Husain**

---

## 4. Folk Paintings of India (Folk/Lok Kala)

Art developed among common people.

### A. Madhubani Art (Bihar)

- Folk art from **Mithila region (Bihar)** & **Terai region (Nepal)**
- Origin legend: First painted during **Lord Rama & Sita's wedding** (Ramayana era)
- Materials: **Rice paste, cow dung, vegetable colors**
- Made during festivals & auspicious occasions
- Primarily by **women**
- Themes: Hindu gods & goddesses, natural elements (sun, moon, trees, flowers, animals, birds)
- **GI Tag** awarded
- Famous painters: **Jagdamba Devi**, **Bhua Devi**, **Bharti Dayal**, **Ganga Devi**

### B. Pattachitra (Odisha)

- Sanskrit: "Patta" = cloth, "Chitra" = painting
- **Classical + folk elements** combined
- Inspired by **Vaishnavism** and **Jagannath culture**
- Painted on **cotton cloth** using natural colors
- Colors sourced from: **burnt coconut shells, hingula, ramaraja, lamp black**
- **No pencil sketch or pen used** — figures outlined directly with brush
- After painting, **lac coating** gives shiny appearance
- Sometimes made on **palm leaves** → called **Tal Pattachitra**

### C. Patua Art (Bengal)

- Folk art of Bengal, started ~**1000 years ago**
- Themes: gods & goddesses
- Painted on **cloth scrolls (pats)**
- Historically: Patua artists traveled singing mythological stories
- Today: used for **social & political campaigns**
- Note: These Hindu-themed paintings are made by **Bengal's Muslim population** — example of **Ganga-Jamuni tehzeeb** (composite culture)

### D. Kalighat Paintings (Kolkata)

- Started in **19th century** by rural migrants near **Kalighat Temple, Kolkata**
- **Water colors** on neutral background
- Early themes: religion & spirituality
- Later shifted to: **social issues**
- Current trends: **women empowerment, romantic, satirical themes**

### E. Patkar Paintings (Jharkhand)

- Made by **Jharkhand's tribal communities**
- Ancient history → known as **one of the oldest schools of painting**
- Themes: Tribal goddess **Maa Mansa**
- Also: religious customs, **non-conventional subjects** (e.g., what happens to human body after death)

### F. Kalamkari (Andhra Pradesh)

- "Kalam" = pen, "Kari" = work
- Painted on **cotton cloth** using bamboo pens
- Colors: mainly **vegetable-extracted**
- Also uses **fermented jaggery water** for coloring
- Practiced in: **Sri Kalahasti** & **Machilipatnam** (Andhra Pradesh)
- **GI Tag** awarded

### G. Warli Paintings (Gujarat & Maharashtra)

- Originated ~**2500–3000 BCE** by the **Warli tribe**
- Resemble **Bhimbetka cave paintings**
- **Central motif:** square/rectangle (chauk/chaukat) surrounded by paintings
- Themes: hunting, farming, dancing, animals, trees, festivals
- Depicts **Palghat** (Goddess of Fertility) & other deities
- Uses **geometric symbols**: triangles, circles, squares
- Painted on **mud or cow dung base**
- Color: **white** (made from gum + rice)
- Well-known tribal art form

### H. Thangka Paintings

- Made in: **Sikkim, Himachal Pradesh, Ladakh, Arunachal Pradesh**
- Dedicated to **Buddhism**
- Painted on **cotton canvas** using bright and shiny colors
- Colors have **philosophical significance:**
  - **Red** → passion & intense emotions
  - **White** → peace
  - **Black** → anger
  - **Green** → human consciousness
- Considered one of the most beautiful & meaningful painting forms

# Indian Music — History, Structure & Types (Summary)

## 📜 Historical Evolution

| Period / Source | Key Detail |
|---|---|
| **Muni (sage)** | Introduced musical art on Earth |
| **Indus Valley Civilization** | 7-folded flute & Ravan Hatha (instrument remnants) found |
| **Vedic Times (~2000 yrs ago)** | First literary musical sources. **Samaveda** mentions all 7 notes of raga *Kharahar Priya* in descending order |
| **Gandharva Veda** | Upaveda of Samaveda; called **Science of Music** |
| **Aitareya Aranyaka** | Mentions **Veena** instrument |
| **Jaiminiya Brahmana** | Describes dance & music |
| **Later Vedic Period** | Ritualistic music: **Sangam** & **Jati Gan** — text lines sung in musical patterns |
| **Bharata Muni — Natyashastra** (200 BC–200 AD) | First major text; describes **22 Shrutis** (keys) and music chapters |
| **Matanga — Brihaddeshi** (9th Century) | First to **define the word "Raga"** |
| **Sarangadeva — Sangeet Ratnakara** (13th Century) | Defines **264 ragas** |
| **Nanda — Sangeet Makarand** (11th Century) | Information on ragas |
| **Rama Matya — Swara Mela Kalanidhi** | Information on ragas |
| **Gurukul System** | Ancient & medieval period — students lived with teachers to learn music |

---

## 🏛️ Anatomy (Structure) of Indian Music

### 3 Pillars: **Raga** + **Tala** + **Swara**

### Swara (Notes / Pitch)
- Originally linked to **Veda recitation**
- Natyashastra: **22 Shrutis**
- Modern Hindustani: **Saptak / Sargam** — 7 notes: **Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni**
- English equivalent: **Pitch**

### Raga
- Sanskrit root **"Ranj"** = to delight / make happy
- **Basis of melody** — evokes a personality, subject, or mood
- Built from notes

#### Types by Number of Notes:
| Type | Notes | Name |
|---|---|---|
| **Odhav** | 5 | Pentatonic |
| **Shadhav** | 6 | Hexatonic |
| **Sampoorna** | 7 | Heptatonic |

#### Types by Purity:
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Shuddha Raga** | Pure — no mixture of another raga |
| **Chhaya Alag Raga** | Mixture of 2 ragas |
| **Sankirna Raga** | Mixture of >2 ragas |

#### Aroha & Avaroha:
- **Aroha**: Ascending notes (each higher than previous)
- **Avaroha**: Descending notes

### 6 Major Ragas (Time & Season based)
| Raga | Time | Season |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhairav** | Dawn | All seasons |
| **Hindol** | Morning | Spring |
| **Deepak** | Night | Summer |
| **Megh** | Late night | Rainy |
| **Shri** | Evening | Winter |
| **Malkosh** | Midnight | Winter |

### Raga Components:

#### 1. Rasa (Emotion evoked) — **Nauras (9 Rasas)**
| Rasa | Emotion |
|---|---|
| **Shringar** | Love |
| **Hasya** | Humor / Laughter |
| **Karuna** | Pathos / Courage |
| **Raudra** | Anger |
| **Bhayanak** | Horror |
| **Veer** | Bravery |
| **Adbhut** | Wonder |
| **Vibhatsa** | Disgust |
| **Shanta** | Peace |

#### 2. Thaat — System to classify ragas into groups
#### 3. Samay — Time of day for performance (raga effective only at prescribed time)

### Tala (Rhythm)
- Rhythmic grouping of beats in a composition
- Range: **3 to 108 beats**
- Used to measure musical **time** (musical meter)
- **Independent** of main music
- **Laya** = Tempo / speed of tala
- Hindustani & Carnatic have different tala systems

---

## 🗂️ Classification of Indian Music (4 Categories)

### 1. 🎵 Classical Music — 2 Types

#### (A) Hindustani Music (North India)
- Diverged from Carnatic in **14th Century**
- Roots: **Natyashastra**
- Focus: Musical structure & improvisation possibilities
- Adopted **Shuddha Swar Saptak** (octave of natural notes) — 8 notes occupying intervals between 2 notes
- **10 Main Singing Styles:** Dhrupad, Dhamar, Hori, Khayal, Tappa, Chaturang, Rag Sagar, Tarana, Sargam, Thumri

##### 🎤 Dhrupad
- **Oldest & grandest** style of Hindustani classical
- Mentioned in Natyashastra
- Name: **Dhruv** (fixed) + **Pad** (poetry) = poetry + singing method
- Established: 13th Century; **Peak under Emperor Akbar**
- Akbar patronized: **Gopal Das, Swami Haridas, Tansen** (Tansen = one of Navratnas)
- **Declined after 18th Century**
- Extended presentation; elaborate raga treatment
- **Dhruv** = unmoving → notes, time, text are fixed
- Starts with **Alap** (wordless), then Dhrupad begins
- Instruments: **Pakhavaj + Tanpura**
- Uses **Sanskrit syllables**
- Performed by **2 people**
- **4 Forms (Vani) / Gharanas:**

| Gharana | Family / Performers | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Dagari Gharana** | Dagar family (Muslims sing Hindu gods/goddesses) | Emphasis on Alap. Ex: Gundecha Brothers (Jaipur) |
| **Darbhanga Gharana** | Malik family — Ram Chatur Malik, Prem Kumar Malik, Siyaram Tiwari | Khand Vani & Gauharbani |
| **Bettiah Gharana** | Mishra family — Indra Kishore Mishra | Nohar & Khand Vani |
| **Talwandi Gharana** | — | — |

##### 🎤 Khayal
- Persian word = **Idea / Imagination**
- Origin credited to **Amir Khusro**
- Based on **short songs of 2–8 lines**
- Composition = **Bandish**
- Greatest patron: **Sultan Mohammad Sharqi** (15th Century)
- Uses **Taana** (instead of Alap) — faster tempo = unique feature
- Theme: Romantic; also sung for divine beings / God / King; **Lord Krishna** has exceptional compositions
- **5 Gharanas:**

| Gharana | Origin / Founders | Key Performers | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gwalior** | — | Nathu Khan, Vishnu Paluskar | Focus: Melody & Rhythm |
| **Kirana** | Kirana Town, UP. Founder: Nayak Gopal. Popularized by: Abdul Karim Khan, Abdul Wahid Khan | Pandit Bhimsen Joshi, Gangubai Hangal | Expression, tuning, note expression |
| **Agra** | Revived by Faiyaz Khan (also called **Rangila Gharana**) | Mohsin Khan Niyazi, Vijay Kichlu | Blend of Khayal & Dhrupad |
| **Patiala** | Under Patiala Maharaja's patronage (19th Century) | Bade Ghulam Ali Khan (greatest vocalists) | Greater rhythm & emotions; intricate taans & alankars |
| **Bhendi Bazar** | Originated 19th Century | — | Breath control training; uses some Carnatic ragas |

##### 🎤 Tarana
- Rhythm plays crucial role
- Short melody repeated in variations
- Uses many words sung at **fast tempo**
- **World's fastest Tarana singer: Pandit Ratan Mohan Sharma** (Mewati Gharana) — title: **Badshah of Tarana**

##### 🎤 Semi-Classical Styles
- Note-based like classical but **lighter versions** of ragas (e.g., Bhopali, Malkosh) + lighter tala + medium/drut laya (faster tempo)
- Emphasis: **Bhaav (emotion) & Lyrics**

| Style | Details |
|---|---|
| **Thumri** | Mixed ragas; romantic/devotional; influenced by **Bhakti Movement**; mostly about Lord Krishna; language: Hindi / Awadhi / Braj Bhasha; usually female voice. **2 types:** Poorvi Thumri (slow tempo), Punjabi Thumri (fast). **Main gharanas:** Banaras, Lucknow. **Famous:** Begum Akhtar |
| **Tappa** | Rhythm important; originated from camel riders' folk songs (NW India); recognized at **Mughal emperor Muhammad Shah's court**. **Unique:** Quick turning of phrases. **Going extinct.** Performers: Mian Sodi, Pt. Laxman Rao (Gwalior), Shanno Khurana |
| **Ghazal** | Poetic form; rhythmic couplets. Origin: **10th Century Iran**. Spread via Islamic sultanates & Sufi saints. Expresses **love despite loss/pain**. Max: **12 Ashaar (couplets)**. Traditional subject: only Love (God or human). Initially only upper class understood. **Famous:** Mohammad Iqbal, Mirza Ghalib, Rumi (13th C), Hafiz (14th C), Kazi Nazrul Islam |

---

#### (B) Carnatic Music (South India)
- Played in **traditional octave**
- Based on **Kriti**; focus on **literary / lyric quality**
- **Kriti** = highly evolved musical song bound to a raga + fixed tala
- **Structure of Carnatic Composition:**

| Part | Description |
|---|---|
| **Pallavi** | 1st or 2nd thematic lines; repeated each stanza — **best part** of composition. **Ragam Thanam Pallavi** — artist gets scope for improvisation |
| **Anupallavi** | Follows Pallavi (2 lines); sung at beginning & sometimes end; not necessarily repeated after every stanza |
| **Varnam** | Sung at beginning of recital to reveal raga to audience. **2 parts:** Purvanga (1st half) + Uttara (2nd half) |
| **Raga Malika** | Usually concluding part of performance; singer fully free to improvise but must return to original theme |
| **Swara Kalpana** | Improvised section; performed at medium & fast pace with drummer |
| **Instrument:** Mridangam | Melody with Mridangam = **Thanam**. Parts without Mridangam = **Ragam** |

# Summary: Indian Music – Folk, Fusion & Modern Music

## 1. Fusion of Classical & Folk Music
- **Bridge:** Devotional music connects royalty (classical) and common people (folk) through worship, creating fusion.
- **Sugam Sangeet** – Devotional genre blending classical + folk; influenced by Prabandh Sangeet and Dhrupad.

### Sub-categories of Sugam Sangeet

#### Bhajan
- **Region:** North India
- **Origin:** Bhakti Movement – saints spread God's message through hymns
- **Features:** Simple lyrics set to melody; sung in one or more ragas; subjects = God/goddess lives, Mahabharata, Ramayana
- **Instruments:** Chimta, Dholak, Duffli, Manjira
- **Exponents:** Tulsidas, Meera Bai, Surdas, Kabir
- **Modern singers:** Anup Jalota, Anuradha Paudwal

#### Shabad
- **Context:** Sung in Gurudwaras in devotion to Sikh Gurus
- **Origin:** Popularized by Guru Nanak & his disciples
- **3 Types:**
  - Raag Shabad singing
  - Traditional Shabad (mentioned in Adi Granth)
  - Lighter ones – no strict rules/ragas

#### Qawwali
- **Purpose:** Sung in praise of Allah, Prophet Muhammad, Islamic saints / Sufis
- **Languages:** Urdu, Punjabi, Hindi + Braj & Awadhi words
- **Venue:** Sufi shrines
- **Composition:** Single raga
- **Performance:** Solo or group; 2 lead singers + 8-member team
- **Instruments:** Tabla, Dholak, Harmonium
- **Structure:** Intensity / tempo builds gradually → signifies devotee reaching transcendent (otherworldly) stage
- **Origin:** Attributed to Amir Khusro (disputed)
- **Famous Qawwals:** Sabri Brothers, Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, Aziz Warsi

#### Gan Sangeet
- **Format:** Choral / group singing
- **Purpose:** Patriotic feelings, protest songs, social message propagation (e.g. stop exploitation of women & children)
- **Example:** Vandemataram (national song)

#### Haveli Sangeet
- **Region:** Rajasthan & Gujarat (now across India)
- **Origin:** Sung in temple premises; now sung outside too
- **Practiced by:** Pushti Marg Sampraday community

---

## 2. Rabindra Sangeet
- **Region:** Bengal
- **Creator:** Nobel laureate Rabindranath Tagore
- **Style:** Mix of classical elements + Bengali folk trends
- **Themes:** Devotion to one true God, nature & its beauty, celebration of life, **patriotism**

---

## 3. Modern Music
- **Key trait:** Complete freedom from traditional conventions (rhythm, melody, etc.)

### Rock Music (Indian Rock)
- Indian elements blended into mainstream rock while retaining global rock features
- Mutual influence: Western rock → Indian rock **AND** Indian classical → Western rock
- **First Indian rock singer:** Usha Uthup

### Jazz in India
- **Origin:** 1920s, Bombay – by African-American jazz musicians
- **Golden Age:** 1930–1950
- Black musicians migrated to India to escape racial discrimination in USA
- **Similarity with Indian classical:** Improvisation evolves naturally in both
- **Fusion born → Indo-Jazz**
- **Pioneers:** Ravi Shankar (sitar), John Coltrane

### Pop Music (Indi-pop / Hindi Pop)
- Indian elements combined with pop
- **Popularized by:** Alisha Chinai (early 1990s)
- **Source:** Mostly from Indian film industry

---

|---|---|
| Veena, Mridangam, Mandolin | Carnatic (major) |
| Flute, Violin | Common to both Hindustani & Carnatic |

# Indian Classical Dance Forms — Summary Notes

## Key Foundational Concepts

- **Classical (Shastriya)**: Dance forms mentioned in ancient texts/shastras
- **Primary Source**: *Natya Shastra* by **Bharat Muni**
- **Evolution period**: Contemporary forms developed from musical plays between **12th–19th century**
- Other sources: inscriptions, genealogies of kings & artists

## Two Major Aspects

| Aspect | Meaning | Symbolism |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| **Lasya** | Grace, emotion, rasa | Feminine features |
| **Tandava** | Movements, rhythm | Masculine features |

## Three Components (Natya, Nritta, Nritya)

1. **Natya** — Dramatic element; imitating characters
2. **Nritta** — Basic dance movements (pure rhythm)
3. **Nritya** — Mudras (gestures) & expressions (abhinaya)

## Nine Rasas (Navarasas)

| # | Rasa | Emotion |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | Shringar | Love |
| 2 | Raudra | Anger |
| 3 | Vibhatsa | Disgust |
| 4 | Veer | Valor |
| 5 | Shant | Peace/calmness |
| 6 | Hasya | Laughter/comic |
| 7 | Karuna | Tragedy/pathos |
| 8 | Bhayanak | Fear |
| 9 | Adbhut | Wonder/amazement |

---

## 8 Recognized Classical Dance Forms

### 1. Bharatanatyam

- **State**: Tamil Nadu (South India)
- **Key Text**: *Abhinaya Darpana* by **Nandikeshwara**
- **Nature**: Solo dance (**Ek-harya**) — one dancer plays multiple roles
- **Movements**: Transitional movements of feet, hips, arms; expressive eyes & hand gestures
- **Orchestra**: Mridangam, Violin, Veena, Flute, Cymbals
- **Conductor**: **Natuvanar**
- **7 parts**: Alarippu → Shabdam → Varnam → Padam → Thalana → Shloka → Jati Swaram
- **Depicted at**: **Chidambaram Temple** gopuram (gate), Tamil Nadu
- **Revived by**: **E. Krishna Iyer** & **Rukmini Devi Arundale**

---

### 2. Kathak

- **Region**: North India
- **Word origin**: *Katha* = story
- **Original context**: Villages/temples — storytellers narrated ancient scriptures
- **Evolution**:
  - **15th–16th century**: Bhakti movement → **Rasleela** folk plays (Radha-Krishna)
  - **Mughal period**: Court performances under Mughal emperors
  - **Nawab Wajid Ali Shah** (Awadh) — major patron
- **Features**: Solo performance; verses recited then executed via movements; emphasis on **footwork**; controlled movements; **Ghungroo** (ankle bells); straight-legged stance
- **Only classical form connected to Hindustani (North Indian) music**
- **Revived by**: **Lady Leela Sokhey** (aka **Menaka**)

---

### 3. Kathakali

- **State**: Kerala
- **Nature**: Synthesis of music, vocal, dance, hand & face gestures
- **Unique element**: Incorporates **ancient Indian martial arts** & athletic traditions of South India
- **Development setting**: Courts & theatres of Hindu principalities (unlike temple/monastery origins of other forms)
- **Influenced by**: **Chakiar Koothu**, **Krishnanattam**, **Koodiyattam**, **Ramanattam**
- **Story source**: Indian epics
- **Costume**: Heavy makeup, large masks, long skirts, large headdresses
- **Face colors**:
  - **Green** = Nobility
  - **Black** = Demonic/evil
  - **Red patches** = Royalty + evil combination
- **Technique**: Hand gestures, facial expressions, eye movements are critical; body weight on outer edges of slightly bent feet
- **Key exponents**: **Raman Kutty Nair** & **Kalamandalam Gopi**

---

### 4. Kuchipudi

- **State**: Andhra Pradesh (Krishna district)
- **Origin**: Village named **Kuchipudi** in Krishna district
- **Also known as**: **Yakshagaan**
- **17th century**: Kuchipudi style envisioned by **Siddhendra Yogi** (guided by **Teerth Narayan Yogi** who composed *Krishna Leela Tarangini* in Sanskrit)
- **Formats**: Dance drama (groups) + solo items
- **Costume**: Elaborate ornaments & jewelry
- **Solo Items**:
  - *Manduk Shabdam* — story of a frog maiden
  - *Bal Gopal Tarang* — dancing with water pot on head, standing on brass plate edges
  - *Tala Chitra Nritya* — drawing pictures with toes while dancing
- **Key exponents**: **Yamini Krishnamurti** & **Raja Reddy**

---

### 5. Mohiniattam

- **State**: Kerala
- **Type**: Classical solo dance for women
- **Name**: *Mohini* = female avatar of Lord Vishnu
- **References**:
  - *Vyavahara Mala* (1709) by **Madamangalam Narayana Namboodiri**
  - *Ghosh Yatra* by poet **Kunjan Nambiar**
- **Shaped into classical format** (18th–19th century) by: **Maharaja Kartika Tirunal** & **Maharaja Swati Tirunal** (Travancore)
- **Movements**: Circular movements, delicate steps, subtle expressions
- **Borrowed from**: *Nangiar Koothu* + folk dances *Kaikottikali* & *Tiruvathirakali*
- **Style**: Elements of both Bharatanatyam (grace) & Kathakali (vigor), but more **erotic, lyrical, delicate**
- **Costume**: Realistic makeup, **Kerala Kasavu saree**
- **Language of lyrics**: **Mani Pravalam** (Tamil + Malayalam + Sanskrit)
- **Key exponents**: **Pallavi Krishnan** & **Sunanda Nair**

---

### 6. Odissi

- **State**: Odisha
- **Themes**: Stories of Vishnu avatars + verses from Jayadev's **Geet Govind**
- **Nature**: Soft dance, soothing lyrics, resembles Bharatanatyam in mudras & emotions
- **Also called**: **Mobile Sculpture**
- **Two main postures**:
  - **Tribhanga** — body deflected at neck, torso & knees
  - **Chauk** — square-like position
- **Key exponents**: **Sonal Mansingh** & **Kelucharan Mahapatra**

---

### 7. Sattriya

- **State**: Assam
- **Introduced**: **15th century AD** by **Shankar Dev** (Vaishnav saint & reformer)
- **Purpose**: Medium for propagating Vaishnav faith
- **Name origin**: Associated with **Sattras** (Vaishnav monasteries)
- **Principles**: Strictly governed rules for hast mudras, footwork, aharya (costume), music
- **Two streams**:
  1. Related to traditional Assamese drama **Bhaona** — from *Gayan Bhanar Nach* to *Khar Manar Nach*
  2. Independent dance numbers: **Chali** (graceful/elegant), **Rajaghadiya Chali**, **Jhumura** (vigorous/majestic), **Nadu**

---

### 8. Manipuri

- **State**: Manipur
- **Origin**: Ancient (pre-recorded history)
- **Context**: Connected to rituals & traditional festivals
- **Legendary references**: Shiva, Parvati & other deities creating the universe
- **Lai Haraoba** — earliest dance form, basis of all stylized dances
  - Meaning: "Merry making of Gods"
  - Ceremonial offering of song & dance
  - Performers: **Maibas** (priests) & **Maibis** (priestesses)
  - Reenacts the creation of the world
- **Rasleela dances**: Originated in **18th century** under **Raja Bhagya Chandra**

---

# Indian Folk Dances — Summary Notes

## General Characteristics of Indian Folk Dances
- **Definition**: Folk art = common property of a group/community; originator forgotten but style preserved across generations
- **Occasions**: Arrival of seasons, childbirth, weddings, festivals, any joyful event
- **Nature**: Simple, spontaneous, no professional training required; performed by locals
- **Performance**: Dancers sing themselves + accompanying musicians with instruments
- **Distinct features**: Each form has unique costume, rhythm, colorful attire with heavy jewelry/designs
- **Cultural role**: Helps understand India's diverse cultural terrain; each state/region has its own folk dance traditions

---

## 1. Chhau (छाऊ)
- **Type**: Tribal martial art dance
- **States**: Odisha, Jharkhand, West Bengal
- **3 Sub-genres**:
  | Genre | State |
  |-------|-------|
  | Purulia Chhau | West Bengal |
  | Seraikela Chhau | Jharkhand |
  | Mayurbhanj Chhau | Odisha |
- **Occasion**: Vasant Utsav (spring festival) — 13 days; Chaitra Parva
- **Performance**: Night time, open space, by male dancers only
- **Style**: Blend of dance + martial arts; employs mock combat techniques
- **Themes**: Hindu mythology, Mahabharata, Ramayana, local folklore, abstract concepts
- **Masks**: Worn in all forms **except** Mayurbhanj Chhau
- **UNESCO**: Inscribed in Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (**2010**)

---

## 2. Kalbelia (कालबेलिया)
- **State**: Rajasthan
- **Community**: Kalbelia tribe
- **Performers**: Only women dance; men play instruments
- **Style**: Movements & costume resemble serpents; signature circular spinning (ghoomna)
- **Reputation**: One of the most **sensuous** folk dance forms
- **Occasion**: Any celebration; integral to Kalbelia culture
- **Instruments**: Khanjari (tambourine), Pungi (snake-charmer instrument)
- **Costume**: Black skirts, traditional tattoos, jewelry, small mirrors, silver-thread embroidered clothes
- **Songs**: Transmit mythological knowledge; demonstrate poetic skill; improvised during performance
- **Tradition**: Purely oral — no texts or training manuals exist
- **UNESCO**: Inscribed in Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity

---

## 3. Bihu (बिहू)
- **State**: Assam
- **Performers**: Both men & women, in groups
- **Earliest evidence**: 9th century depictions in Tezpur & Darrang regions; 14th century inscriptions of Chutia King Lakshmi Narayan
- **Names**: Bihu Naas (dance), Bihu Geet (music)
- **Named after**: Bohag Bihu (Rongali Bihu) — Assamese New Year
- **3 Types of Bihu**:
  | Type | Month | Nature |
  |------|-------|--------|
  | **Rongali / Bohag Bihu** | April | Spring festival, most prominent |
  | **Kongali / Kati Bihu** | October | Somber, scarce season |
  | **Bhogali / Magh Bihu** | January | Harvest festival, community feast |
- **Style**: Colorful traditional costumes, group formations, fast hand & foot movements
- **Ethnic groups associated**: Kaivartas, Deuris, Sonowal Kacharis, Chutiyas, Boros, Misings, Rabhas, Morans, Barahis

---

## 4. Ghoomar (घूमर)
- **Also called**: Gangaur
- **State**: Rajasthan
- **Origin**: Bhil tribe — performed to worship Goddess Saraswati; later adopted by other Rajasthani communities
- **Performers**: Women only (veiled/ghunghat, wearing ghaghra)
- **History**: Originally local women of Rajputana → later adopted by Rajput noble women; men were **not allowed to watch**
- **Popularized**: During Rajput kings' reign
- **Occasion**: Auspicious occasions
- **Regional variations**:
  - Gujarat border districts: **Fast tempo**, Garba-like steps
  - Dholpuri / Braj region: **Slow tempo**
  - Udaipur, Kota, Bundi: Variations in costume & style

---

## 5. Dollu Kunitha (डोलू कुनिता)
- **State**: Karnataka
- **Energetic drum dance** — performed during major festivals & celebrations
- **Deity**: Sri Sri Veeralingeshwara (form of Lord Shiva); dollu (drum) associated with Shiva
- **Legend**: Shiva made a drum from skins of slain demons; Kuruba Gowda community celebrates by drumming
- **Origin**: Kuruba Gowda community rituals, North Karnataka
- **Performance**: 10–12 drummers; both men & women; circular or semi-circular formation
- **Events**: Karga festival, temple processions, Mysuru Dasara, Jamboo Savari, Bengaluru Habba, temple car festivals

---

## 6. Kolattam (कोल अट्टम)
- **States**: Tamil Nadu & Andhra Pradesh
- **Also called**: Kolannal, Kol Kolannal
- **Etymology**: "Kol" = small stick, "Attam" = play/dance
- **Antiquity**: Mentioned in Kanchipuram as "Chay Vak Aa Kol Attam"
- **Legend**: Demon Basavasura was enchanted by young girls performing Kolattam with sticks; event commemorated as **Kola Jathara** festival
- **Performers**: Only women
- **Group size**: 8 to 40 dancers
- **Pinal Kolattam**: Variation using **ropes** instead of sticks (colorful, visually striking)
- **Duration**: 10 days, starting after Diwali on Amavasya (new moon) night
- **Also performed** in other Indian states under different names

---

## 7. Padayani (पड़नी)
- **State**: Kerala
- **Location**: Bhadrakali temples on banks of Pampa River
- **Type**: Ritualistic art form
- **Legend**: After killing demon Daraka, Goddess Bhadrakali's rage wouldn't subside; Shiva's servants (Bhuta Ganas) danced before her to calm her down
- **Masks (Kolam)**: Made from areca nut palm leaves (Erik Nat); entirely natural colors — green (wood), kari (black), manjal (yellow), podi, sindoor
- **Performance locations**: Kadammanitta, Kottangal, Thara, Kunnam, Thanam, many South Kerala temples
- **Kolam types**: Ganapati, Yakshi, Pakshi, Madan, Kalan, Murdha, Pishacha, Bhairavi, Gandharva, Mukalam
- **Community activity**: Villagers participate in collecting areca leaves & making kolams
- **Educates**: About historical, cultural, and ancient events of the region

---

## 8. Pata Kunitha (पटक निधा)
- **State**: Karnataka (Mysore region)
- **Performers**: Mainly men
- **Type**: Devotional dance; religious significance; focus on rhythm & talent, minimal commentary
- **Group size**: 10–15 people
- **Props**: Long bamboo poles called **"Pata"** (10–15 ft long), decorated with colorful ribbons, silver/brass umbrellas
- **Visual**: Unique, vibrant, energetic spectacle
- **Appeal**: Popular across all religions

---

# Indian Theatre — Summary Notes

## What is Theatre
- Composition of multiple skills: dance, acting, singing, talking, mime + theatre crafts (masks, makeup, costumes)
- Collective expression for entertainment & social education
- Commonly called **Drama**

---

## 1. CLASSICAL SANSKRIT THEATRE

### Origins & Treatises
- Oldest amphitheaters: **Sita Bena & Jogi Mara caves** (excavated ruins)
- **Natya Shastra** by **Bharat Muni** (~2nd century AD) — first formal treatise on dramaturgy
- Legend: Lord Brahma created **Natya Veda** by combining elements of the 4 Vedas
- Natya Shastra describes **10 types of plays** (1-act to 10-acts)

### Types of Ancient Plays
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Lok Dharmi** | Realistic depiction of people's lives |
| **Natya Dharmi** | Conventional plays using narration, style, symbolism |

### 10 Play Categories (Rupakas)
Ank, Bhan, Dima, Tham, Gran, Natak, Prahasana, Prakaran, Svak, Vidi, Vyayog  
*(Only **Natak** & **Prakaran** detailed in Natya Shastra)*

### Conventions of Classical Sanskrit Plays
- Mostly 4–7 acts
- Always **happy ending** (tragedy extremely rare)
- Male protagonist always achieves his goal
- Well-defined: opening → progression → development → pause → conclusion

### Key Playwrights & Plays
| Playwright | Period | Notable Work(s) |
|------------|--------|-----------------|
| **Ashwaghosha** | — | **Sariputra Prakarana** (9-act; first known classical Sanskrit drama) |
| **Bhasa** | 4th–5th cent. BC | 13 plays composed |
| **Shudraka** | — | **Mrichchhakatika** (The Little Clay Cart) |
| **Vishakhadatta** | — | **Mudra Rakshasa** |
| **Bhavabhuti** | — | **Mahavir Charitra** |
| **Harshavardhana** | — | **Ratnavali** |
| **Kalidasa** | — | **Malavika Agnimitra**, **Vikramorvashi**, **Abhigyana Shakuntalam** |

### Decline of Sanskrit Theatre
- Diversion toward poetry
- Rigidity → restricted creative space
- Declining popularity of Sanskrit
- Muslim ruler invasions

### Surviving Classical Form
- **Koodiyattam / Koothiyattam** (Kerala) — follows all Natya Shastra rules
- Performed by **Chakyar** & **Nambiyar** castes
- Languages: Sanskrit, Prakrit, Malayalam
- Background music: **Maddalam** & **Edakka**
- Leading exponent: **Margi Madhu Chakyar**

---

## 2. FOLK THEATRE

- Rich tradition across India's regions
- Depicts local lifestyle, social norms, beliefs, customs
- Rural roots, rustic flavor
- Most forms emerged **15th–16th cent. AD** with devotional themes
- Later adapted love ballads & local hero stories

### Three Sub-Types:
- A. Ritual Theatre
- B. Theatre of Entertainment
- C. South Indian Theatre

---

### A. RITUAL THEATRE
Popular during **Bhakti movement** — expression of faith in God.

| Form | State/Region | Key Details |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Ankiya Nat** | Assam | Mythological one-act play; started by **Shankar Deva** & disciple **Mahadeva** (16th cent. AD); opera-style; Krishna's life; Sutradhara + **Gayan Bayan Mandali** (musicians with **Khol** & cymbals); uses **masks** for expressions |
| **Kala** | Assam | Vaishnavite; branches: **Dashavatar Kala**, **Gopal Kala**, **Galan Kala** |
| **Ramlila** | Uttar Pradesh | Enacts Ramayana; performed before **Dussehra**; male actors only (incl. female roles) |
| **Raslila** | Gujarat | Dance-drama on Radha-Krishna love story |
| **Bhuta** | Kerala (Kannara dist.) | "Spirit" — traditional practice of appeasing dead ancestors |
| **Ramman** | Uttarakhand (Garhwal) | Ritual theatre; villagers offer to **Bhumiya Devta** in temple courtyard; **UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage**; key aspect: **Jagar singing** (musical rendition of local legends) |

---

### B. THEATRE OF ENTERTAINMENT
Secular stories of love, valor, socio-cultural themes. Aim: entertain rural masses.

| Form | State/Region | Key Details |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Bhavai** | Rajasthan & Gujarat | Semi-classical music; instruments: **Bhungala**, **Jhanjha**, **Tabla**; Sutradhara called **Nayak** |
| **Das Kathiyai** | Karnataka | Two narrators: **Gayak** (main singer) + **Palia** (co-narrator); wooden instrument = **Kathiya** |
| **Garodia** | Gujarat (Garodia community) | Uses **painted pictures** to narrate romance & valor |
| **Jatra** | Eastern India (Bengal) | Open-air; initiated by Vaishnavaites to spread Krishna's teachings |
| **Karala** | Himachal Pradesh (foothills) | Open-air theatre form |
| **Maanch** | MP (Malwa region) | Dialogues in couplets = **Rangat Dohas** |
| **Swang** | Punjab & Haryana | Music drama; instruments: **Ek Tara**, **Harmonium**, **Sarangi**, **Dholak**, **Kharta** |
| **Nautanki** | North India | Offshoot of Swang; **most popular North Indian theatre**; historical/social/traditional stories; dialogues lyrical with **Nagada** drum beats |
| **Ojapali** | Assam | Associated with **Manasa** (serpent goddess) festival; 3 parts: **Baniya Khand**, **Bhatiyali Khand**, **Dev Khand**; **Oja** = narrator, **Paliz** = chorus |

# Indian Puppetry — Summary Notes

## What is Puppetry?
- **Puppetry (कठपुतली)** = Folk art where puppets enact stories as a play
- Puppets are colorful, beautifully decorated dolls managed by a **Sutradhar** (string-holder)
- Entertains while showcasing culture & childhood nostalgia

## Origin & History
- Practiced for **thousands of years** in India
- **Harappa & Mohenjo-daro** excavation sites yielded thread-connected puppets → evidence of puppetry in Indus Valley Civilization
- **Tamil classical literature *Shilappadikaram*** (1st–2nd BC) mentions puppets
- **Philosophical significance in Bhagavad Gita**: Krishna as Sutradhar (puppeteer), humans as puppets; Krishna called **Sutradhar**
- The puppet handler is also called **Sutradhar** — connecting Mahabharata era to modern tradition

## Four Broad Categories of Indian Puppetry

---

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Material | Wood |
| Height | 8–9 inches |
| Coloring | Oil paint |
| Body | Thin wooden pipes for limbs |
| Attire | Traditional Indian outfits |
| Accessories | Jewelry, ornaments for realistic look |
| Control | Strings attached → Sutradhar holds & moves them |

### Types of String Puppets

### a) Kathputli — Rajasthan
- **Kath** (wood) + **Putli** (doll) = wooden doll
- **No legs** → strings attached to hands only
- Dressed in bright Rajasthani outfits + heavy jewelry

### b) Kundhei — Odisha
- Made of **light wood**

### c) Gombe Aata — Karnataka
- Ancient folk art of Karnataka

- Facial features & outfits recreated like Yakshagana dancers

### d) Bommalattam — Tamil Nadu
- **Tallest & heaviest** puppets in India
- Height: **4–5 feet**; Weight: **~10 kg**
- Strings attached to an **iron ring**

- Depicts **mythological stories** (religious significance)

# Summary — History of Coins and Currency in India

## Foundations

- **Money** = common medium of exchange for goods & services
- **Currency** = money issued by a central government (India → Rupees, USA → Dollars)
- Forms of currency: Coins → Paper Currency → Digital Currency → Crypto Currency

## Pre-Currency: Barter System

- Goods exchanged directly for goods (e.g., food grains for cloth)
- Problems: double coincidence of wants, no standard valuation, time/effort heavy
- Barter used in **Prehistoric Age** (Paleolithic, Mesolithic, Neolithic) — no currency evidence; society was hunting-gathering, limited exchange

## Indus Valley / Harappan Civilization (3500–1300 BC)

- First evidence of currency-like system in India
- Long-distance trade with **Mesopotamian Civilization** (present-day Iraq)
- No coins recovered — speculated use of **stone seals** for trading
- Harappan seals found in Iraq/Syria region
- Harappan coins question **still unresolved** until script is deciphered
- Some historians believe barter was used, but barter for long-distance trade seems impractical

## Vedic Age (1500–600 BC)

- No conclusive evidence of coins/currency
- **Rigvedic people**: pastoral, dependent on animals & animal products
- **Later Vedic people**: agriculturalists
- Trade limited and mostly in **barter form**

## Mahajanapada Period (circa 600 BC) — Punch-Marked Coins

- First conclusive evidence of currency use in India
- **16 Mahajanapadas** ruling (Magadha, Kashi, Avanti, Vajji key ones)
- **Buddhist texts** and **Panini's Ashtadhyayi** mention copper & silver coins
- Called **Karshapana** or **Pana**
- Irregular shape; symbols punched onto them → **Punch-Marked Coins**
- Symbols varied by Mahajanapada:
  - Saurashtra → **bull** symbol
  - Panchala → **swastika** symbol

## Mauryan Empire — Punch-Marked Coins Peak

- First all-India empire → trade & economic activity boomed
- Common currency essential; massive issuance of punch-marked coins
- Used ~450 different symbols:
  - **Natural symbols**: sun, moon, hills, trees
  - **Geometric figures**: circle, rectangle, wheel
  - **Human figures & animals**

## Indo-Greeks — Die-Struck Coins & Portraits

- First to issue **die-struck coins** in India (molds used in mints → uniform shape)
- **Mint** = place where metals melted & shaped into coins
- First to use **portraits** on coins
  - e.g., King **Menander** shown at various life stages

## Kushana Dynasty (1st–4th Century AD) — Gold Coins

- Ruled North India
- First to issue **large quantities of gold coins**
- Coin features:
  - **Obverse**: King's portrait, name, title
  - **Reverse**: King's favorite deity/god
- Used **Brahmi** language on coins

## Gupta Empire — Sanskrit on Coins & Dinara

- First to use **Sanskrit** on coins
- Issued abundant **gold coins** called **Dinara**
- Some kings showed artistic side: **Samudragupta** & **Kumaragupta** depicted playing **veena**

## Delhi Sultanate (till end of 12th Century AD)

- Coins inspired by **Islamic history & culture**
- Turkish rulers replaced royal Indian designs with **Islamic calligraphy**
- Islamic culture prohibited human figures/portraits on coins
- Metals used: **Gold, Silver, Copper**
- **Higher value coin**: **Tanka**
- **Lower value coin**: **Jittal**

## Sher Shah Suri (1540–1555) — Tri-Metallic System

- Ruled only 15 years but introduced landmark currency reform
- **Tri-Metallic System** standardised:
  | Metal | Coin Name | Standard Weight |
  |-------|-----------|----------------|
  | Gold | **Mohar** | 169 grams |
  | Silver | **Rupee (Rupaya)** | 178 grams |
  | Copper | **Dam** | — |
- 1 Silver Rupee = **40 Copper Dam**
- Sher Shah's **Rupee** format continued till early 20th century — **precursor to modern Rupee**

## Mughal Empire (from 1526)

- Unified & standardised currency system — **copied Sher Shah Suri's tri-metallic system**
- Continued Mohar, Rupee, Dam structure

## British Rule & Independent India

- **1717**: British got permission from Mughal ruler **Farrukhsiyar** to mint Mughal coins
  - Gold coin → **Carolina**
  - Silver coin → **Angelina**
  - Copper coin → **Cupperoon**
- After **Battle of Plassey (1757)** & **Battle of Buxar (1764)**, British became de facto rulers
- Still used Mughal money but no longer needed Mughal permission to mint
- **Post-1857 Revolt**: Rupee declared official currency; Mughal calligraphy replaced with portrait of **King George V**
- **1935**: **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** established to manage currency

## Post-Independence Currency Reforms

- **15 August 1950**: **Anna System** introduced — first indigenous coinage system of independent India
  - 1 Rupee = **16 Annas**
  - British king portrait removed → replaced by **Ashoka's Lion Capital**
- **1956 (Coinage Amendment Act)**: **Decimal Coin System** introduced
  - 1 Rupee = **100 Paise**
  - Old Anna System → **Purana Rupee** (old rupee)
  - New Decimal System → **Naya Rupee** (new rupee)

## Paper Currency in India

- First paper currency issued by British Government in **1861**
- Denominations: ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹500, ₹2000
- Advantages: easy to carry, low issuance cost
- Higher denominations → paper currency
- Lower denominations (₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10) → coins

## Digital & Crypto Currency

- **21st Century**: Digital India age
- Money transferred digitally via mobile & internet
- Digital wallets functionally similar to physical wallets but enable easy long-distance transfer
- **Cryptocurrency**: decentralized (no central government issuance)
  - Created by programmers solving complex calculations ("mining")
  - Supporters claim it will reform global currency system & prevent government manipulation
  - Rapid adoption in India


# Calendars of India – Study Notes

## Why Calendars Were Invented
- Settled life (post hunting-gathering) created need for **timekeeping**
- Required for: trade, festivals, religious gatherings, administration
- Calendar = solution to record days, months, years
- Calendar development in India was **linked to astronomy**

## History of Calendar-Making in India
- **Vedic Age** – origin point. **Jyotisha** (one of the six Vedangas) studied positions of Sun, Moon, Earth, constellations to determine time
- Purpose: find **auspicious (shubh)** positions of celestial bodies for Vedic rituals
- **Aryabhata** in *Aryabhatiya* calculated solar year as: **365 days, 6 hours, 12 minutes, 3.84 seconds**
  - Modern scientific value: **365 days, 5 hours, 59 minutes, 16 seconds** – remarkably close
- By **Medieval Age**: ~**30 different calendars** existed across India, varying by region, history, culture

## Three Methods to Define a Year

| System | Basis | Duration | Notes |
|--------|-------|----------|-------|
| **Solar Year** | Earth's revolution around Sun | 365 d 5 h 5 min 16 s | Calendar year = 365 days; leftover time added via different methods per calendar |
| **Lunar Year** | Moon's revolution around Earth | 354 days (29.5 × 12) | 11 days shorter than solar year |
| **Luni-Solar Year** | Year = solar cycle, months = moon cycle | Combination | Best of both |

- **Adhika Maas** (extra month) added every **2.5 years** to sync lunar calendar with solar cycle → keeps seasons & festivals fixed

## Common Features Across Indian Calendars
- Month names are **similar**: Chaitra, Vaishakha, Jyeshtha, Ashadha, Shravana, Bhadrapada, Ashwina, Kartika, Margashirsha, Pausha, Magha, Phalguna
- Regional variations exist: e.g. Vaishakha = **Bohag** (Assamese) / **Bośāk** (Bengali)

---

| Property | Detail |

| **Alternate NY** | Some regions celebrate Kartik 1 (October) as New Year |

### Month Division
- Each month → **2 fortnights (pakshas)**:
  - **Shukla Paksha** (Bright half): starts day after Amavasya, ends on Purnima (full moon)
  - **Krishna Paksha** (Dark half): starts day after Purnima, ends on Amavasya
- **New month starts from Krishna Paksha**
- **Adhika Maas** added every 2.5 years to sync with solar cycle

### 12 Months of Vikram Calendar

# Summary: Indian Literature - Ancient Period

## INTRODUCTION
- Literature = mirror of society; reflects conditions, helps understand past & ancestors' perspectives
- Indian literary tradition = one of world's oldest
- Oldest surviving text = **Vedas** (Sanskrit); foundation stone of Sanatan Dharma & Indian civilization
- Non-religious literature also flourished (Kalidasa's dramas & poetry)
- Literary history = unbroken chain connecting past to present

## LANGUAGES FOCUSED
- **Sanskrit, Pali, Prakrit, Dravidian languages**
- **Sanskrit**: world's oldest surviving languages; developed from ~1500 BC (Vedic Age)
- Sanskrit = Mother of all Indo-Aryan languages; influenced Prakrit, Pali, Dravidian, Hindi, Bengali, etc.

---

## VEDAS
- **"Veda"** = knowledge; divine/eternal in Sanatan Dharma
- Not restricted to any sect/geography; treats universe as one family
- Chantings/prayers for peace, prosperity, health, happiness
- **Composed**: 3000 BC – 1000 BC; orally transmitted for generations
- **Written compilation**: ~600 BC
- **Four types**:

### 1. RIGVEDA
- Oldest Veda; **1028 individual Sanskrit hymns**
- Oldest surviving composition of any Indo-Aryan language
- Themes: life, death, creation, sacrifice
- **Chief deity**: Indra (Rain God) — most hymns dedicated to him
- Other gods: Agni (fire), Varuna (water), Rudra (wind & storm), Aditya (sun), Vayu (air), Ashwini Twins
- Female gods: Usha (dawn), Prithvi (earth), Vak (speech)
- Society was nature-worshipping

### 2. YAJURVEDA
- **"Yajur"** = sacrifice
- Mantras & rituals for sacrifices
- Called **"Ritual Veda"** — guidebook for priests/rishis conducting sacrifices

### 3. SAMAVEDA
- **"Sama"** = melody/songs
- **1875 hymns** arranged as musical ragas & raginis
- Called **"Book of Chants"**
- Proof that Indian music developed in the Vedic Age

### 4. ATHARVAVEDA
- Also called **"Brahma Veda"**
- Credited to rishis **Atharva & Angira**
- Focus: daily life peace & prosperity + treatment for **999 diseases** (via mantras & kriyas)
- Sometimes called "book of black magic"

### VEDANGAS (6 limbs/branches of Vedas)
1. **Shiksha** — education/phonetics
2. **Nirukta** — etymology
3. **Chhanda** — metrics/prosody
4. **Jyotisha** — astronomy
5. **Vyakarana** — grammar
6. **(also Shiksha listed earlier)**
- **Panini's Ashtadhyayi** (~400 BC) = best Vedanga example; defines Sanskrit grammar rules

### BRAHMANAS & ARANYAKAS (Vedic commentaries)
- Provide detailed explanation of Vedic mantras
- **Brahmanas**: Vedic rituals & sacrifices detail
- **Aranyakas**: abstract topics — soul, birth, death, beyond
- Final part of Aranyakas = **Upanishads**

---

## UPANISHADS
- **"Upa-ni-shad"** = sitting near the guru (represents Guru-Shishya parampara)
- Last part of Vedas → called **"Vedanta"**
- Philosophical questions: life, death, salvation
- ~200 written; **108 popular**
- Focus: right knowledge & beliefs (NOT rituals)
- **Popular Upanishads**: Kathopanishad, Mundaka Upanishad, Shwetashwatara Upanishad
- **"Satyameva Jayate"** (Truth Alone Triumphs) — from Upanishads, inscribed in India's national emblem

---

## PURANAS
- Ancient Indian mythological texts
- Stories: universe formation to destruction
- Main focus: **Hindu Trinity** — Brahma, Vishnu, Mahesh (Shiva)
- **18 Puranas** — each tells story of an important god; explains philosophical/religious concepts
- Written in simple language/story format → more popular among common people than Vedas
- Gave rise to **Upapuranas** (minor Puranas)

---

## RAMAYANA
- Multiple versions; most popular = **Valmiki's Ramayana**
- **"Adi Kavya"** (earliest epic); Valmiki = **"Adi Kavi"** (earliest poet)
- **24,000 verses**; divided into **7 Kandas (parts)**
- Each Kanda depicts important events from Lord Ram & Sita's life
- **Yuddha Kanda**: Ram's victory over demon king Ravana; Sita rescued from Lanka
- Represents triumph of good over evil
- Through Ram's life (Maryada Purushottam), teaches **4 Purusharthas**: Dharma, Artha, Kama, Moksha

---

## MAHABHARATA
- Most popular version = **Vedavyasa's**
- Originally **8,800 verses** → called **"Jaya"**
- Expanded to **24,000 verses** → called **"Bharata"** (after ancient Vedic tribe Bharat)
- Current form: **1 lakh (100,000) verses** → **"Mahabharata"**
- Conflict: **Kauravas vs Pandavas**
- Main narrator: **Shri Krishna** (avatar of Vishnu)
- Contains **Bhagavad Gita**: Krishna's message to Arjuna before war
  - Topics: Dharma, Karma, righteous living
  - Explained for society's benefit

---

## NON-RELIGIOUS SANSKRIT LITERATURE

### DRAMA
- **Popular playwrights**: Kalidasa, Sudraka, Vaishakh Datta, Bhavabhuti, Bhasa
- **Kalidasa's Abhijnana Shakuntalam** (Recognition of Shakuntala) — most popular Sanskrit drama
  - Story: King Dushyanta & Shakuntala → meet in forest, separated, reunited via son **Bharat**
  - King Bharat → founded **Bharat Dynasty** → descendants = **Bharatvanshi**
  - India's Hindi name **"Bharat"** derives from King Bharat
- **Vikramorvasiyam** (Urvashi)
- **Malavikagnimitram** (love story of Malavika & Agnimitra)

### POETRY (Kalidasa)
- Kumarasambhava, Raghuvansha, Meghadootam, Ritusambhara

### OTHER POETS
- **Hari Sen**: poems on Samudragupta's bravery; included in Allahabad Pillar Inscription

### BOOKS OF LAW (500 BC – 200 BC)
- **Dharma Sutras & Dharma Shastras**: rules governing Hindu society
  - Property transfer, punishments for crimes
- **Manusmriti**: describes roles of men & women; supports caste-based society with different punishments for different castes

### OTHER KEY WORKS
- **Kautilya's Arthashastra**: economics, diplomacy, statecraft

---

## PALI & PRAKRIT LITERATURE (Post-Vedic Age)
- Buddhists & Jains made these languages prominent → became languages of common people
- **Buddha gave sermons in Pali**

### BUDDHIST LITERATURE
- **Tripitakas** (in Pali):
  1. **Vinaya Pitaka** — daily life rules & regulations for monks
  2. **Sutta Pitaka** — morality & Dhamma (dharma)
  3. **Abhidhamma Pitaka** — philosophy & physics
- **Jataka Stories**: compilation of Buddha's **previous 550 births**
  - Uses popular myths, folklore, moral stories
  - Message of righteous living

### JAIN LITERATURE
- Jain Tirthankars used **Prakrit** to spread philosophy
- **Anga, Upanga, Parikrama** — most popular Jain texts in Prakrit

---

## DRAVIDIAN LITERATURE
- Four major Dravidian languages: **Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Kannada**
- **Tamil**: oldest; closest to Sanskrit in grammar & root words

### TAMIL LITERATURE
- **Sangam Literature** (300 BC – 300 AD)
  - ~**2381 poems** by **473 poets**
  - "Sangam" = assemblies organized by **Pandya kings in Madurai**
  - Two fields:
    - **Akam** (inner): love, sexual relationships, abstract emotions
    - **Puram** (outer): social life, ethics, valour, customs
- **Tiruvalluvar's Thirukkural (Kural)**
  - Topics: politics, governance, love, morality
  - Called **"Tamil Vedas"**
- **Two major Tamil epics**:
  - **Silappadikaram** (Story of an Anklet) — by **Ilango Adigal**
  - **Manimekalai** — by **Shattan**
- **Bhakti movement (9th–12th century)**:
  - **Alwars**: Vishnu worshippers — devotional poetry in Tamil
  - **Nayanars**: Shiva worshippers — devotional poetry in Tamil

### MALAYALAM LITERATURE
- Spoken in Kerala & adjoining areas; youngest Dravidian language

# Indian Literature — Medieval & Modern Period (11th Century Onwards)

## Big Picture Trends (Post-10th Century)

- **Two new trends from 11th century** shaped Indian literature:
  1. Turk & Mongol settlement → cultural/linguistic influence (Persian, Urdu)
  2. Bhakti & Sufi movements → rise of regional languages & devotional literature
- **British colonial period (18th–19th century)**: introduced modern printing press; Europeans translated Indian works into European languages (e.g. Charles Wilkins translated Bhagavad Gita into English, 1785)
- **Printing press** made literature accessible to the masses → **nationalist leaders** used literature to spread nationalism & fight British rule

---

## 1. Persian Literature

- Persian & Sanskrit both evolved from a common **Indo-European** language
- **Introduced by Turks & Mongols** in 12th century AD
- **Court language** of Delhi Sultanate & Mughals
- **Amir Khusro** (1253–1325) — earliest Persian poets in India; disciple of Nizamuddin Auliya
  - Poetry collection: **Diwan**
  - Works: *Nuh Siphr*, *Masnavi Deval Rani Khizr Khan* (Alauddin Khilji's son + Rajput princess Deval Rani)
- **Sultanate Historians:**
  - **Ziauddin Barani**: *Tarikh-i-Firoz Shahi* (history till Firoz Shah Tughlaq), *Fatwa-i-Jahandari* (political values for Muslim rulers)
  - **Minhaj-us-Siraj**
- **Mughal Period (zenith of Persian):**
  - **Babur**: *Tuzuk-i-Baburi* (autobiography in Turkish; translated to Persian by Akbar)
  - **Gulbadan Banu Begum** (Humayun's sister): *Humayun Nama* (Humayun's struggles to recapture throne)
  - **Abul Fazl** (Akbar's court historian): *Ain-i-Akbari*, *Akbar Nama* (key sources on Mughal society & polity)
  - Akbar commissioned translation of Sanskrit works into Persian: Ramayana, Mahabharata (Persian trans. = **Razmnama**), Upanishads
  - **Jahangir**: *Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri* (autobiography in Persian)
  - *Shah Jahan Nama*, *Alamgir Nama* (describe Shah Jahan & Aurangzeb's reigns)
- **Decline after Aurangzeb's death (1707)** → Persian replaced by Urdu
- **Last Mughal ruler Bahadur Shah Zafar** — famous Urdu poet (pen name: **Zafar**)

---

## 2. Urdu Literature

- **Origin**: interaction of Turkish (Persian-speaking) soldiers & Indian (Hindi-speaking) soldiers in Sultanate army barracks
- **Grammar** follows Hindi; **script** follows Persian
- First adopted in **Deccan region** — Bahmani states (Ahmednagar, Golconda, Bijapur, Berar) → called **Dakkhani**
- **14th century**: Amir Khusro wrote in both Persian & Urdu
- **Golden period: 18th–19th century** — became common people's language
- Key poets: **Mirza Ghalib**, **Sauda**, **Dard**, **Mir Taqi Mir**
- **Sir Syed Ahmad Khan** (20th c.): reforms in Muslim society through Urdu literature
- **Mohammad Iqbal**: *Bang-i-Dara* (poetry collection); wrote *Sare Jahan se Achha Hindustan Hamara*
- **Premchand** wrote in Urdu before Hindi

---

## 3. Hindi Literature

- **Evolution**: Apabhramsha → Hindi (7th–14th century AD); Apabhramsha itself evolved from Prakrit
- **Bhakti Movement (from 11th c.)** → key driver of Hindi & other regional languages
- **Major Dialects:**
  - **Braj Bhasha** — Braj region, UP
  - **Awadhi** — Awadh region, UP
  - **Bhojpuri, Magadhi, Maithili** — Bihar
  - **Khari Boli** — Delhi & surroundings (most popular form; Urdu influence visible)
  - **Rajasthani** — Rajasthan (considered Hindi dialect)
- **Bhakti Poets & their languages:**
  - **Tulsidas** — *Ramcharitmanas* (Awadhi)
  - **Surdas** & **Bihari** — Braj Bhasha
  - **Mirabai** — Rajasthani
  - **Kabir** — multiple dialects (Awadhi, Braj, + Persian influence)
- **Nationalist period (British rule):** shift from devotional → nationalist themes; Sanskrit works translated to Hindi to reconnect with roots
- **Bharatendu Harishchandra** (19th c.): *Andher Nagari* (City of Darkness), *Bharat Durdasha* (called to overthrow British)
- **Swami Dayanand Saraswati**: *Satyarth Prakash*
- **Munshi Premchand** (Novel King/Upanyas Samrat): *Godan*, *Gaban*, *Rangbhoomi*
- **Other 20th c. writers:**
  - **Suryakant Tripathi Nirala**
  - **Maithili Sharan Gupt**
  - **Sumitranandan Pant**
  - **Ramdhari Singh Dinkar**
  - **Harivansh Rai Bachchan**
  - **Mahadevi Verma** — women's issues; awarded **Padma Vibhushan**

# Languages of India — Summary Notes

## 1. India's Language Diversity (Census 2011)
- **19,500** mother tongues; **121** major languages (≥10,000 speakers each)
- Hindi alone has **≥40 dialects/mother tongues**: Bhojpuri, Magahi, Braj Bhasha, Haryanvi, etc.
- Proverb: *"Kos kos par badle paani, chaar kos par baani"* — water changes every mile, language every few miles.

## 2. Three Major Language Families of India

| Family | % of Population | Regions |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| **Indo-Aryan** | ~74% | North, Central, West, East India |
| **Dravidian** | ~25% | South India |
| **Sino-Tibetan** | ~0.6% | North & Northeast frontiers |

---

## 3. Indo-Aryan Family

### Origin & Scope
- Subgroup of larger **Indo-European** family (spoken across Central Asia & Europe)
- Brought by **Aryans** migrating into India; evolved through local interaction
- Ancient: **Sanskrit, Prakrit, Pali** → Modern: **Hindi, Gujarati, Bengali, Marathi, Punjabi, Assamese, Rajasthani**, etc.

### Sanskrit
- Developed from **Vedic Age (~1900 BC)** — oldest continuously developed Indian language
- **Vedas, Upanishads, Puranas, Dharmashastras** composed in Sanskrit
- **Panini's *Ashtadhyayi* (~400 BC)** — grammar rules still unchanged after 2000+ years
- **Golden Phase: Gupta Period** — Kalidasa (*Abhijnana Shakuntalam*, *Meghadootam*), Vishakhadatta, Shudraka
- Became **language of the educated elite** (Brahmins & Kings); declined among common people

### Prakrit & Pali
- **Prakrit** (~600–1000 AD): Not a single language but a **group** of common people's languages in *Mahajanapadas*
- **Magadhi / Ardha-Magadhi**: Spoken in Magadha; **Mahavira's Jain texts** composed in it
- **Pali**: Derived from Prakrit; **Buddha's sermons** in Pali
  - Three key Buddhist texts: **Vinaya Pitaka, Sutta Pitaka, Abhidhamma Pitaka**
- **Apabhramsha** (8th–9th C AD): Transitional dialects before modern Indo-Aryan languages; **Hindi & Bengali emerged from Apabhramsha**

### Modern Indo-Aryan Languages (post-1000 AD)
- **Hindi, Assamese, Bengali, Gujarati, Marathi, Punjabi, Rajasthani**
- Hindi lexicon draws from **Sanskrit + Apabhramsha + English + Urdu**
- **~44%** of India's population speaks/understands Hindi today

- **Indigenous** to India — minimal outside influence (unlike Indo-Aryan)

## 7. Constitutional Provisions — Official Languages

### Article 343(1)
- **Official language of the Union**: Hindi in **Devanagari script**
- **English** permitted for all official purposes for **15 years** (until **26 Jan 1965**)

### Official Languages Act, 1963
- **English granted status of "subsidiary official language"** (after protests from non-Hindi states)
- Union government work can be done in **both Hindi and English**

### Eighth Schedule — Scheduled Languages
- Listed in **8th Schedule** of the Constitution
- **States free to choose their own official language**
- **Initially 14 languages** listed (transcript cuts off before full list; today there are **22**)
- Existence of **22 Scheduled Languages** + many non-scheduled languages with active literature

### Key Debate (1947)
- North India wanted **Hindi as national language**; South India disagreed
- Tribals also had rich language histories
- **Constitution makers chose middle path** — no single "national language"; instead **Official Language + Subsidiary Official Language + Scheduled Languages**
- Language nationalism seen as divisive (example: Europe splitting into small countries along language lines)


# Martial Arts of India — Summary Notes

## 1. Kalari Payattu (Kerala)
- **Oldest martial art in the world**; originated in Kerala around 4th century CE
- Spread to Southeast Asia, Japan, China — influenced Jujitsu, Kung Fu, Taekwondo, Karate
- **Originator**: Guru Parshuram (as per scriptures)
- **Kalari** = Malayalam word for the special training ground (pit dug 4–5 ft, filled with soft soil)
- **4 Stages**:
  1. **Mai Payattu** — body conditioning, joint & muscle stretching
  2. **Kol Thari Payattu** — wooden weapons (long canes, short canes, *Otta* = animal-horn-shaped bent weapon)
  3. **Ankathar Payattu** — sharp weapons: sword & shield, spear, dagger, **Urumi** (flexible sword worn at waist like a belt, hidden, used in emergencies)
  4. **Verum Kai** — bare-handed defense against armed/unarmed opponents
- **Golden Age**: Practiced by kings in gurukuls; each had a squad of Kalari practitioners
- **Decline**: Banned by British in 1804 after the **Kerala Arm Revolt**; possession of weapons led to imprisonment
- **Post-Independence**: Revival efforts; now developed as a competitive sport
- **Today**: Not part of warfare but excellent for physical fitness & self-defense

## 2. Silambam (Tamil Region)
- Also one of the oldest martial art forms
- Geographical proximity to Kerala → practices similar to Kalari Payattu
- **Originator**: Sage Agastya (Rishi Agastya)
- **History**: Dates to at least 4th century BCE (per Sangam literature); used for self-defense & against wild animals
- **Chera, Chola, Pandya** dynasties developed organized warfare form; kings trained in gurukuls & maintained Silambam fighter squads
- 18th century: Successfully used against British but lost due to superior British arms, ammunition & naval power
- **Banned** after British occupation; revived post-Independence as martial art & sport
- **Main weapon**: **Bamboo stick** — flexible, does not break in combat
- **Variation**: **Torch Silambam** — burning cotton cloth tied to both ends of bamboo stick
- **Other weapons**: *Aruva* (sickle), *Vaal* (curved sword), *Katti* (knife)
- In sports form, mostly limited to bamboo stick

## 3. Paari Khanda (Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha)
- **Paari** = sword; **Khanda** = shield
- Main weapons are **sword and shield**
- Practice is very similar to **Chhau dance**
- Chhau dance is believed to be **derived from Paari Khanda**

## 4. Thoda (Himachal Pradesh)
- Originated in Himachal Pradesh; mix of martial art, sport & culture
- Played during **Baisakhi (April)**
- Tests **archery skills** of players
- **Main equipment**: Bow & arrows (made of wood)
- **Inspiration**: Archery duels during Mahabharata times in Kullu & Manali valleys
- **Bow length**: 1.5m – 2m (proportional to player height)
- Equipment made by **traditional artisans**
- **Two teams**: *Pashi* and *Sathi* — considered descendants of **Pandavas** and **Kauravas**
- Up to **500 members per team**; some are dancers (not fighters) who boost morale
- Not just martial art — reflects **spirituality**, celebrated as a **festival**, promotes **community bonding**

## 5. Gatka (Punjab — Sikh)
- Performed by **Sikhs of Punjab**
- **Origin**: After 5th Guru **Guru Arjan Dev** was killed by Mughals in 1606; his son **Guru Hargobind** started it to defend the community
- **17th century**: **Guru Gobind Singh** (himself a weapons expert) developed & gave it final form
- Earlier used in combat & war; now practiced for **physical fitness & self-defense**
- Practiced **with and without weapons**
- **Main weapons**:
  - *Soti* — long wooden stick
  - *Tega* — long broad sword
  - *Chakkar* — round weapon with wooden balls at ends
- When played as a sport, movements performed to beats of **dhol & nagada**
- Performed in **large processions** on: Guru Nanak's birthday, Guru Gobind Singh Jayanti

## 6. Thang-Ta (Manipur)
- **Thang** = sword; **Ta** = spear → main weapons
- Used by Manipuri kings to fight
- **Banned** when Manipur came under British control; revived post-Independence
- **3 forms of practice today**:
  1. **Ritualistic** — linked to Tantric practices
  2. **Performance** — beautiful spear & sword dance
  3. **Actual fighting**

## 7. Mardani Khel (Maharashtra)
- Traditional martial art of Maharashtra
- **Origin**: Maratha guerrilla warfare against Mughals
- Weapons & movements designed for **Western Ghat terrain** — fast movements, rapid attacks in tough mountain passes with minimal weapons
- **Weapons**: *Lathi* (stick), *Sword*, *Shield* (Dhal-Talwar), *Khanda* (double-edged straight sword), *Dand-Patta* (double-edged flexible sword)
- **Banned** by British; transformed into a **sport** — "Mardani Khel"
- **Unique feature**: Men and women trained together with same weapons, treated equally

## 8. Masti Yuddha (Varanasi / Banaras)
- Popular in **Varanasi** and surrounding areas
- Very old origin; mentioned in the **Ramayana**
- **Masti Yuddha** = from two Sanskrit words

# UNESCO World Heritage Sites in India — Study Notes

## UNESCO — Background
- **UNESCO**: United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization
- **Established**: 1945 — post-WWII (to promote peace, cultural exchange, dialogue)
- **Objective**: World peace via international cooperation in education, science, culture
- **Trigger event**: 1960 — Abu Simbel Temple (Egypt) threatened by dam; UNESCO launched international rescue mission → global awareness of endangered heritage
- **1972**: UNESCO World Heritage Convention formed
- **World Heritage Committee**: 21 members; selects & categorises sites

## Three Categories
| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Cultural** | Human civilisation significance |
| **Natural** | Nature preservation significance |
| **Mixed** | Both cultural & natural significance |

- Global attention on site
- Easier government & NGO funding for preservation
- Protected under Geneva Convention & Hague Convention for Protection of Cultural Sites (even during war/armed conflict)

# Schools of Philosophy — Summary Notes

## 1. Introduction: Philosophy in India

- **Darshan Shastra** = Indian term for philosophy (darshan = perspective/way of seeing)
- Philosophical questions addressed: Who created the universe? Is there another world? What is dharma/adharma?
- **Two major traditions** based on Veda acceptance:

| Tradition | Belief | Schools |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| **Orthodox (Astika)** | Accept Veda authority; Veda are infallible/supreme | Sankhya, Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Yoga, Purva Mimamsa, Vedanta (Uttara Mimamsa) |
| **Heterodox (Nastika)** | Reject Veda authority | Jainism, Buddhism, Ajivika, Charvaka |

---

## 2. Common Principles Across All Six Orthodox Schools

1. **Dharma ≠ Religion** — Dharma = Code of Conduct (universal, applies to both theist & atheist)
2. **Dharma gives life meaning** — separates humans from animals (who are controlled by sense organs). Through dharma, one achieves **Purusharthas**:
   - **Dharma** → **Artha** → **Kama** → **Moksha** (merging with Paramatma)
3. **Logic, reasoning & analysis** used to address metaphysical/spiritual issues — not blind faith

---

## 3. The Six Orthodox Schools

### 3.1 Sankhya School
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Founder** | Kapila Muni |
| **Text** | Sankhya Sutra |
| **Core Philosophy** | **Dualism (Dvaitavada)** — universe divided into **Purusha** & **Prakriti** |
| **Purusha** | Consciousness/Chaitanya; Soul (Atma) & Mind; **Intangible** (cannot be touched/seen, only felt) |
| **Prakriti** | **Tangible** — can be seen/felt via sense organs; includes all living & non-living things |
| **Jiva (life) birth** | When Purusha attaches to Prakriti through **Desires (Kamana/Ichha)** |
| **Maya** | These desires fulfilled via sense organs (eye, ear, nose, skin, tongue) |
| **Death & Moksha** | Purusha separates from Prakriti → merges into **Cosmic Consciousness**. This state = **Moksha/Salvation** |
| **Key Points** | ❌ Denies existence of God/Superhuman. ❌ Silent on reincarnation. ✅ Right knowledge → Moksha |

---

### 3.2 Nyaya School
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Founder** | Akshapada Gautama |
| **Text** | Nyaya Sutra |
| **Core Philosophy** | **Logic, Reasoning & Analysis** — most rational school of Hindu philosophy |
| **Four Pillars of Valid Knowledge** | 1. **Perception (Anubhuti)** 2. **Inference (Anuman)** 3. **Comparison (Tulana)** 4. **Testimony (Sakshya)** |
| **Comparison** | Similar to Socrates & Plato (Ancient Greece) |
| **God** | ✅ Believes in God — God created, sustains, and destroys the universe |
| **Path to Moksha** | Use **logical thinking** |

---

### 3.3 Vaisheshika School
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Founder** | Rishi Kanada |
| **Core Philosophy** | **Realistic & Objective** — universe made of **Atoms (Anu)** |
| **God/Brahman** | Fundamental force that gives **consciousness to atoms** |
| **Atomic theory** | Atoms → Molecules → Elements |
| **Five Elements (Pancha Mahabhuta)** | **Prithvi** (Earth) — **Vayu** (Air) — **Jal** (Water) — **Agni** (Fire) — **Akash** (Ether/Space) |
| **Significance** | Major role in early Physics (Bhautik Vigyan) in Indian subcontinent |
| **Relation to Nyaya** | Closely associated; later merged with Nyaya (both believed in scientific reasoning) |
| **God** | ✅ Believes in God/Cosmic Power as sutradhar (controller) |

---

### 3.4 Yoga School
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Founder** | Rishi Patanjali |
| **Text** | Yoga Sutra (~2nd century BC) — considered origin of Yoga |
| **Core Idea** | Combination of **Physical, Mental & Spiritual practice + Discipline** |
| **Goal** | **Meditation & Yogic techniques** → separate Purusha from Prakriti → attain **Moksha/Salvation** |
| **Eight Limbs (Ashtanga)** | 1. **Yama** (self-control) 2. **Niyama** (observance of rules) 3. **Asana** (fixed postures) 4. **Pranayama** (breath control) 5. **Pratyahara** (choosing an object/withdrawal) 6. **Dharana** (fixing the mind) 7. **Dhyana** (concentrating/meditation) 8. **Samadhi** (complete dissolution of self, mind & object merge) |
| **Later Influence** | Became part of Buddhism & Jainism too |
| **UN Recognition** | **International Yoga Day** — June 21 (annually) |

---

### 3.5 Purva Mimamsa School
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Founder** | Rishi Jaimini |
| **Literal Meaning** | Mimamsa = Reasoning, Interpretation & Application |
| **Focus** | **Ritualistic part of Vedas** — Samhita & Brahmana portions |
| **Core Belief** | Vedas contain **Eternal Truth**; Moksha/Swarga requires fulfilling **all duties prescribed in Vedas** |
| **Emphasis** | Not just performing rituals, but also understanding the **justification & reasoning** behind them |
| **Social Impact** | Encouraged **Priestly class (Brahmins)** → created social distance between Brahmins & common people |
| **Problem** | Only priestly class could read/interpret Vedas; common people were uneducated & lacked access → dependent on priests for every ritual |

---

### 3.6 Uttara Mimamsa / Vedanta School
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Nature** | Most **important & philosophically advanced** Orthodox school; precursor to **Bhakti Movement** (Medieval age) |
| **Key Difference from Purva Mimamsa** | Purva Mimamsa focuses on **Samhita & Brahmana** (early Veda parts); Vedanta focuses on **Upanishads** (end of Vedas) |
| **Four Parts of Vedas** | 1. Brahmana 2. Samhita 3. Aranyaka 4. **Upanishad** (last = Vedanta = end of Veda) |
| **Oldest Text** | **Brahma Sutra** (2nd century AD) |
| **Core Philosophy** | **Brahma/God = Ultimate Truth**; everything else = **Maya (illusion)** |
| **Three Basic Hindu Texts** | 1. **Upanishads** 2. **Brahma Sutras** 3. **Bhagavad Gita** |
| **Later Development** | Many sub-schools of Vedanta developed from 8th century AD onward |

---

# Bhakti & Sufi Movement — Study Notes

## Ⅰ. FOUNDATIONAL PHILOSOPHERS (Pre-Bhakti)

| Philosopher | Century | Philosophy | Core Belief |
|-------------|---------|------------|-------------|
| **Adi Shankaracharya** | ~8th C | **Advaita Vedanta** (Non-Dualism) | Atma & Parmatma are one. Established 4 Maths: Badrinath, Shringeri, Jagannath Puri, Dwarka. |
| **Ramanuja** | 12th C | **Vishishtadvaita** (Qualified Monism) | Atma cannot fully merge with Parmatma; only those with true knowledge can unite. |
| **Madhavacharya** | 13th C | **Dvaita** (Dualism) | Atma & Parmatma are separate entities. Salvation through devotion to God. |

> **Swami Vivekananda** later propagated Advaita Vedanta in the West.

---

## Ⅱ. BHATKI MOVEMENT — INTRODUCTION

- **Period:** 6th–12th Century AD, started in **Tamil Nadu** (South India)
- **Pre-Bhakti Hinduism:** dominated by Brahminical rituals, sacrifices (yajnas), Sanskrit exclusivity — inaccessible to common people
- **Bhakti alternative:** Personal devotion (bhakti) to God → sufficient for salvation; elaborate rituals unnecessary
- **Tools:** Bhajans, kirtans, poetry in local/common languages
- **Stance on Caste:** Opposed caste system; open to all (especially lower castes)
- **Key Groups:**
  - **Nayanars** → devotees of **Shiva**
  - **Alwars** → devotees of **Vishnu**

### Spread to North India → Two Streams

| Stream | God | Key Saints |
|--------|-----|------------|
| **Nirguna** | Formless (Nirakar) | Kabir, Guru Nanak |
| **Saguna** | With form (Sakar) | Tulsidas, Surdas, Meera, Chaitanya Mahaprabhu |

Saguna further split into:
- **Vaishnav** (Vishnu / Ram / Krishna)
- **Shaiv** (Shiva)
- **Shakta** (Devi / Shakti — popular in East & North-East India)

---

## Ⅲ. NIRGUNA SAINTS

### Kabir
- One of the most important Nirguna saints
- **Background:** Abandoned by Brahmin widow mother; raised by poor Muslim weaver family
- **Beliefs:**
  - Formless God — rejected **idol worship**, **yajnas/havans** (Hindu), **namaz** (Muslim)
  - All religions refer to the same one God (Ram, Hari, Govind, Allah, Rahim, Khuda)
  - Only path: **personal devotion**; external religious aspects irrelevant
- **Social critique:** Challenged narrow-minded societal norms through forceful, direct poetry
- **Legacy:**
  - His dohas (couplets) reflect unity of God
  - After death, Hindus & Muslims both claimed his body
  - Ideas incorporated into **Adi Granth Sahib** (Sikh holy book)
  - Followers formed **Kabir Panthi** sect

### Guru Nanak (1469–1539)
- Nirguna / Nirakar God worshipper
- Not intended to create a new religion; followers later formed **Sikhism**
- **Born:** Talwandi (Nankana Sahib, now Pakistan)
- **Personality:** Simplicity + Peacefulness; helped poor & needy
- **Objective:** Remove corrupt & degrading practices from society
- **Social Reforms:**
  - Advocated **social equality** (against caste divisions)
  - Advocated for **women's condition**
- **Legacy:** Teachings compiled in **Guru Granth Sahib** (Sikh holy book)

---

## Ⅳ. SAGUNA SAINTS

### Surdas
- **Disciple of:** Vallabhacharya
- **Work:** Wrote **Sur Sagar** — recollections of Krishna's childhood & youth with deep affection
- Blind, yet composed vivid devotional poetry

### Meera Bai
- Widowed at young age; considered **Shri Krishna as her husband**
- Wrote numerous devotional poems; still sung in Vrindavan

### Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (1486–1534) — Eastern India
- **Vaishnavite:** Krishna = highest form of God (not just Vishnu's incarnation)
- **Sankirtan:** congregational chanting in temples, homes, streets
- **Hare Rama Hare Krishna** movement; core founder of **ISKCON** (International Society for Krishna Consciousness, est. New York 1966)

### Ramanand (1400–1470)
- Popularized **Ram worship** in North India
- Women & outcasts allowed in his gatherings
- **Kabir is considered his disciple**

### Tulsidas (1523–1623)
- Wrote **Ramcharitmanas** (epic poem on Ram's life)
- Foremost Ram-bhakta

---

## Ⅴ. IMPORTANCE OF BHATKI MOVEMENT

| Aspect | Impact |
|--------|--------|

| **Philosophy** | Broadly **humanistic** — love & equality for all beings |
| **Social reform** | Saints spoke against social evils |

## Ⅵ. SUFI MOVEMENT

- **Context:** Islamic orthodoxy (Ulemas) and rising materialism in Sultanate period
- **Response:** Liberal tradition within Islam → **Sufism**
- **Core message:** Simple life, devotion to God, **Ganga-Jamuni Tehzeeb** (composite culture), **Hindu-Muslim unity**

### Etymology of "Sufi"
- **Suf** (wool) → coarse woolen clothes worn by Sufis
- OR **Safa** (purity) → reflects purity of Sufi saints

### Timeline
- By **11th century**: well-developed movement with its own literature & practices

### Organization

| Term | Meaning |

| **Khanqah** | Place where Sufi teacher & disciples lived |
| **Sheikh / Pir / Murshid** | Sufi teacher |
| **Murid** | Disciple |

# Summary: Spread of Indian Culture Abroad

## Core Idea
- India has been in contact with the outside world since the 3rd millennium BC (Harappan–Mesopotamian trade)
- Despite natural barriers (Himalayas in north, oceans on 3 sides), cultural exchange flourished
- Indian cultural influence visible today in South Asia, SE Asia, China, Japan, Korea, Central Asia
- Buddhism originated in India and spread to these regions
- Indian influence also seen in food, clothing, dance, festivals, daily life
- Unlike forced conversions elsewhere (Crusades, holy wars), Indian culture spread through **spiritualism, tolerance, and respect** for all cultures
- Adopting cultures **modified** Indian traditions per local geographic/historic conditions (e.g., Buddhism adapted locally in Tibet, China, Japan)

---

## Means of Spread of Indian Culture

| Factor | Role |
|--------|------|
| **Traders** | Travelled long distances; west to Rome, east to China; in 1st C BC went to Cambodia, Java, Sumatra, Malay Islands (called *Suvarna Dvipa* - "Islands of Gold") |
| **Scholars / Universities** | Nalanda (east) & Vallabhi (west) were major cultural interaction centers; Xuan Zang described Nalanda as 7-story with 1500 teachers & ~10,000 students |
| **Monks / Missionaries** | Spread Buddhism to Tibet, China, Bhutan via Vikramashila & Dantapuri universities |
| **Wanderers / Gypsies (Roma)** | Travelled via land routes through Punjab → Sindh → Multan → Persia → Europe |

---

## Roma (Romani) People — Indian Origins

- Originated from **Northern Indian states**
- Legend: Alexander took them back after his Indian campaign
- Skilled weapon manufacturers & iron workers — high demand in armies
- Route: Sindh → Persia → Turkey → Europe
- Present in ~30 European countries; max population in **Turkey**
- **Romani language** shares numerals with Hindi: *ek, duj, trin, štar, panč* = एक, दो, तीन, चार, पांच
- Cultural parallels with India: white = mourning, mehendi on hands, similar rituals/taboos
- One of the most persecuted tribes in Europe; many hide Indian roots due to discrimination

## Region-Wise Spread

### 1. Central Asia
- **Present-day countries**: Turkmenistan, Tajikistan, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan
- **Silk Road**: Started from Xi'an (China) → crisscrossed Central Asia → Europe; Indian traders accessed via Nepal-Tibet-Kashmir
- Carried goods + scholars + monks → spread Buddhism
- Remains of Buddhist stupas, monasteries, temples, statues found across Central Asia
- **Kingdom of Khotan** (present-day Chinese Xinjiang): 2000+ year old cultural link with India; coins had Chinese on front, **Prakrit** on back; monasteries had Sanskrit manuscripts

### 2. East Asia

#### China
- Contact from **2nd C BC**
- **Kashyapa Matanga** & **Dharmarakshita** (monk-scholars) invited by Han Emperor **Ming Ti** (67 AD) → introduced Buddhism; White Horse Monastery built for them
- Buddhism rapidly became popular → large number of Chinese Buddhist pilgrims came to India
- **Xuan Zang** (7th C AD): travelogues are key source for Harshavardhana's reign
- **Bodhidharma** (from Kanchipuram, studied at Nalanda): popularized Yoga & Meditation (Dhyana) in China; Meditation = "Chan" in Chinese, derived from Sanskrit **Dhyāna**
- **Wei Dynasty** (4th C AD): declared Buddhism **state religion**; thousands of Sanskrit texts translated to Chinese
- Chinese Buddhists built **rock-cut caves** similar to Ajanta & Ellora — e.g., **Dunhuang**, **Yungang**, **Longmen** caves; Yungang declared UNESCO World Heritage Site (1983)

#### Korea & Japan
- Buddhism spread from China → Korea → Japan
- Reached Japan ~525 AD via Korean emperor who sent Buddha statue, ritual objects, sacred texts with a friendship mission
- Journey: India → China → Korea → Japan took **at least 100 years**
- Conflict between native **Kami worshipers** (Shinto) & Buddhists; Buddhism prevailed due to **imperial sponsorship**
- Japan today: ~84 million Buddhist followers

#### Tibet
- Located on plateau north of Himalayas; mostly Buddhist today
- Buddhism became major religion by end of **8th C AD**
- Spread due to **Vikramashila & Nalanda** universities
- King **Thisong Detsen** invited two scholars from Nalanda:
  - **Santarakshita**: built first Buddhist monastery in Tibet
  - **Padmasambhava**: known for spiritual & magical powers; worshiped as Bodhisattva in Tibet

### 3. Sri Lanka

- Earliest contact evidence in **Ramayana** (demon king Ravana)
- Historically: **Mauryan king Ashoka** played key role — sent son **Mahendra** & daughter **Sanghamitra** to spread Buddhism
- Sri Lankan king **Devanampiya Tissa** converted → Buddhism became stronghold
- **Pali language** (popular in India) used in Sri Lanka; major Buddhist texts *Dipavamsa* & *Mahavamsa* written in Pali
- **Sinhalese language** = mixture of Pali + Tamil + Sanskrit
- Indian food, painting, dance, music also influenced Sri Lanka

### 4. Southeast Asia

#### Myanmar (Burma)
- Indian traders from Tamralipti & Amaravati ports travelled via land route to China
- Gradual Buddhist influence; King **Aniruddha** (10th C AD) united regions, promoted Buddhism
- Today ~80% population is Buddhist

#### Thailand
- Indian cultural influence from **1st C AD** onwards via traders, teachers, missionaries
- Buddhism wholeheartedly accepted
- Remains of **Vaishnava temples** found — evidence of Sanatana Dharma followers in the past

#### Cambodia
- Cultural & historical relations **2000+ years**
- Sanskrit name: **Kamboja**
- **Kaundinya dynasty** (Indian origin) ruled; Kaundinya prince from **Ganjam, Odisha** migrated & established first ruling dynasty
- Predominantly Buddhist today but retains **Hindu rituals, murti puja, Indian mythological aspects**
- **Khmer language** uses Sanskrit words extensively
- Hindu-Buddhist Indian temple architecture influenced construction
- **Angkor Wat**: originally dedicated to Lord Vishnu, later converted to Buddhist temple — living example of Indian cultural influence

#### Indonesia & Malaysia
- Ancient rulers adopted Indian culture (Indianized kingdoms)
- **Kedah province (Malaysia)**: Shiva statues excavated
- By medieval era, most population converted to **Islam** but retained Hindu roots
- **Deepavali & Dussehra** still celebrated in many parts
- **Indonesia**: Largest Shiva temple on Java island — **Prambanan**; nearby temples for Vishnu & Brahma, each with vahana shrines (Nandi, Garuda, Hamsa)
- Prambanan decorated with Ramayana & Mahabharata sculptures; built by **Sailendra Dynasty** (8th C AD); now **UNESCO World Heritage Site**
- **Bali island**: predominantly Hindu (80% residents) — statues of Shiva, Ganesha, Durga, Buddha found; Holi, Deepavali, Dussehra very popular; rest of Indonesia converted to Islam

### 5. Arab World

- Earliest trade links from **Harappan civilization**
- Two routes: Land & Sea; **Sea route cheaper/safer** due to monsoon winds
  - Summer: monsoon blows toward India → traders came from Arabia to India
  - Before winter: wind direction reverses → ships easily returned to Arabian Peninsula
- Monsoon winds were a **blessing** for India-Arab trade
- Arab world emerged as **transit hub** for India–Europe trade
- After Islam spread in West Asia, trade intensified
- **9th C AD onwards**: Travelers like **Sulaiman, Al-Masudi, Al-Idrisi** documented flourishing trade

#### Scientific & Cultural Exchange
- Indian scientific works first popular in Arab world → then reached Europe via Arabs
- **Astronomy**: *Brahma-sphuta-siddhanta* & *Khandakhadyaka* translated as *Sindhind* & *Arkand*; works of **Aryabhata** & **Varahamihira** translated to Arabic
- **Mathematics**: **Decimal number system** — biggest contribution; Europeans called it "Arabic numerals" but Arabs acknowledged Indian origins

### 6. Rome (Roman Empire)

- Contact very old; trade flourished during **Kushana rule** (1st C AD)
- High-demand Indian goods in Europe:
  - Pepper, betel nut, spices, ivory, silk, muslin, precious stones
  - Spices valued **higher than gold & silver**
  - Pepper & ginger sold at **100x original Indian price**
- **Pliny** (Roman historian) documented this
- **Vasco da Gama's** 1498 discovery of direct sea route to India was driven by **spice trade** — Arabs acted as intermediaries, Europeans wanted to break Arab monopoly
- **Chola dynasty** ports: **Kaveripattinam** & **Mamallapuram** were major trading hubs to Europe
- Chola kings installed **lighthouses** at ports for ship guidance
- European merchants & ambassadors settled in South Indian towns
- Tamil kings recruited European bodyguards called **Yavanas**, especially for ship protection
- European sea adventuring ultimately culminated in **colonialism in India**
